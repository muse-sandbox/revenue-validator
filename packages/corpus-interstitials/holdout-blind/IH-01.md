# IH-01 — Scratch-to-reveal coupon interstitial for new free users

## Product context
The Ultimate Guitar app monetizes free users through a layer of locally served full-screen interstitial paywalls. At the time of this experiment the local interstitial layer was static: shown at most once per day (taking the place of an ad interstitial, and otherwise only as a fallback), always selling the same standard intro offer for the premium tier (first year at 19.99, renewing at 39.99), with no segmentation, no contextual targeting, and essentially no history of being A/B tested as a standalone monetization channel. This experiment is the first iteration of a broader program to treat local interstitials as a tunable monetization surface — varying frequency, creative format, and offer — without hurting retention or cannibalizing existing paying flows. The specific iteration tested here changes the creative format of the interstitial shown to brand-new free users on first entry into the tab screen.

## Pre-launch research & rationale
The team's pre-launch confidence work covered three strands:

1. **Audience behavior data (internal, iOS ad-eligible users, weekly uniques).** Users whose subscription had expired kept returning to the tab surface steadily for at least two weeks after expiration (~120–150 unique returners per week for each of days 1–14 after expiration, of whom ~45–70 were shown an ad interstitial). Tab-view intensity in the same cohort was long-tailed: 303 users viewed 1 tab/day, 214 viewed 2, declining to ~38 at 14 tabs/day. Ad-interstitial exposure was heavily skewed toward zero: 625 users saw no ad interstitial at all, 319 saw exactly one, 134 saw two, and almost nobody saw more than five. The read: many entries into the tab surface are currently not monetized by any interstitial at all.
2. **Prior experiments on the same layer (earlier projects, described generically).** A test that inserted a two-step ad-free-themed pre-paywall after every ad interstitial failed badly — a steep drop-off on the first pre-paywall screen pushed end-to-end source conversion to roughly 0.07%, versus a modeled ~+10% access uplift. In contrast, a test replacing the usual ad interstitial with a short promotional video produced a statistically significant ARPU gain with no visible retention damage and was adopted in production; its learnings were that a short branded video with a clear free-trial message beats static images, but needs strict frequency capping and careful triggering. A separate earlier effort had also shown winback-style interstitials aimed at former subscribers to be a promising direction. Overall lesson shaping this iteration: creative/format changes on this surface can move conversion materially, and interaction-heavy formats may outperform static screens.
3. **Open program questions.** The pitch listed open questions on optimal frequency and timing, whether gamified first screens (e.g. a swipe/scratch-to-reveal mechanic) lift conversion or paying-traffic quality, whether variable creatives raise conversion without raising cancellations, whether the intro price could be raised or the intro period shortened, and why ex-premium users convert unusually well from interstitials.

A formal reach & impact model was not filled in for this iteration (the page's reach/impact and power sections remained template placeholders).

## Hypothesis
As stated pre-launch (program-level): applying a more systematic, data-driven configuration of local interstitial paywalls (coverage, timing, frequency, content, and offers) for ad-monetized users is expected to increase ARPU of this audience by XX% (the expected effect size was left unquantified in the document) without a statistically significant drop in 7-day retention — the rationale being better balancing of subscription vs. ad monetization, elimination of wasted impressions, and better matching of paywall content to user context. For this iteration specifically, the tested mechanism is that a gamified "lottery ticket" reveal of a discount should convert better than the static interstitial.

## Mechanics
- **Placement:** replaces the standard local interstitial shown on entry to the tab screen.
- **Trigger:** shown when the user first enters the tab; a 5-second timer runs before the interstitial can be skipped (in one arm).
- **Timing / Frequency:** shown only once per user (first tab entry only).
- **Audience segment:** new users only — those who completed the install tour during the experiment — and only fully free users (no current or past subscription).
- **Platforms:** iOS and Android.
- **Offer-SKU structure:** same products as the usual interstitials; the only change is the presentation. The screen shows an initially hidden promo code that the user "rubs off" like a lottery ticket (with device vibration during scratching and a celebratory animation on reveal), after which a paywall opens with the revealed discount applied automatically — no code entry required.

## Experiment design
- **Arms (3):** A — control, current static interstitial; B — scratch-to-reveal coupon interstitial with the 5-second timer after which it can be skipped; C — identical to B but with no timer and no option to skip the interstitial.
- **Primary focus:** subscription conversion and revenue from the interstitial source; program-level guardrails were retention and cannibalization of other paying flows (per the pitch), with tab-viewing engagement also tracked.
- **Analysis segments:** all free users; and, separately, users whose subscription originated from an interstitial source excluding winback-type interstitials.
- **Power / MDE:** no formal power calculation was documented pre-launch (the design table was left as an unfilled template).
- **Instrumentation plan:** new events for coupon scratch start, coupon view, and coupon apply-click; the existing paywall/purchase funnel events extended with a promo-code parameter and a new source value for this interstitial type.
