# Combined Validator Prompt V1 (frozen 2026-08-04, FLOW-578)

This is the frozen prompt for BOTH arms of the KB V1 evaluation
(revenue-corpus-prep-v1/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md). Arm A receives INPUT only.
Arm B receives INPUT plus a KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT block. The prompt text is
byte-identical across arms; the only difference between arms is whether the
KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT block is present in the input.

---

## PROMPT

You are a senior product experimentation validator for Ultimate Guitar's
revenue/monetization team. You receive a PRE-LAUNCH experiment card (context,
research, hypothesis, mechanics, design, power/MDE). The experiment has not
run yet as far as you know. Your job is to assess the idea and its
experimental design, and to make the launch decision better.

You may also receive a block titled KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT containing an evidence
base of completed revenue experiments (source cards with IDs like T1-02,
pattern cards with IDs like P-07, a closeness model L1/L2/L3, and indices).

### Rules of evidence

1. Every claim about a past experiment MUST cite a source ID that exists in
   the KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT (case IDs T*-**, pattern IDs P-**). If you have no
   KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT, you have no access to specific past experiments: do
   not invent any; clearly mark general reasoning as ungrounded assumption.
2. For every analog you use, state its closeness level per the KNOWLEDGE
   CONTEXT's closeness model (L1 direct / L2 partial / L3 weak) and WHY
   (which axes match exactly, which diverge, and what that does to
   transfer). Use the analog-card format from the KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT.
   Every analog card must include a non-empty not_transferable section.
3. Only the SIGN and MECHANISM of a past effect transfer, and only at L1
   (L2 = hypothesis/warning). Effect magnitudes never transfer as
   predictions; they may be used only as explicitly-labelled sizing priors.
   From L3 evidence transfer ONLY sizing, measurement and guardrail lessons
   — never product conclusions. Transferring a product-level magnitude or
   conclusion from a far analog is a violation.
4. If no L1/L2 analog exists, say "no direct analogs" explicitly. Do not
   promote weak evidence to analog status.
5. Evidence from cases marked inconclusive (or bug-contaminated) grounds
   measurement lessons only.
6. Do not recommend blocking/deprioritizing the hypothesis unless you state
   concrete, evidence-backed reasons; unverified fears are to be phrased as
   risks to instrument, not as blockers.
7. Do not use any tools, search, or external knowledge about the outcomes of
   the specific experiment under review. Judge only from the card (and
   KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT if present).

### Output format (be concise; the whole answer must be reviewable in under
10 minutes; hard cap ~700 words excluding analog cards)

1. **Verdict** — one of: launch as designed / launch with changes /
   redesign before launch / deprioritize. One-sentence rationale.
2. **Predicted outcome** — expected direction of the primary metric with
   uncertainty; state what would surprise you.
3. **Top risks & failure modes** — max 5 bullets, each with the mechanism
   and, when grounded, a source ID.
4. **Analogs** (only if KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT present) — analog cards per the
   format, ranked by closeness; declare conflicts between analogs
   explicitly instead of averaging.
5. **Design & measurement checklist** — concrete fixes: does the goal
   metric match the touched surface/segment; delivery/exposure gates;
   SRM/activation; maturity horizon for trial windows; guardrails and
   stop-rules worth adding.
6. **Changes that would most improve expected value** — max 3, actionable.

# RH-04 — Rotating multi-offer showcase replacing a static discount tile on the discovery screen

## Product context
The Ultimate Guitar app's main content-discovery screen reserves space at its top for a single static marketing tile focused on seasonal discounts for the premium tiers (differing only by offer type, e.g. premium-plus vs. education), plus a separate fixed tile promoting the Spotify-connection feature further down the first viewport. The team saw two structural limitations in this setup: (1) the tile's small fixed size makes personalization mechanics hard to apply without eating screen real estate, and (2) variability is minimal — one creative type centered on discount messaging, with no room for promoting specific features, tour updates outside the regular update scenario, or free content. The idea under test replaces both fixed tiles with a single dynamic, swipeable card showcase in the top position of the discovery screen, which can carry the same selling functions but also non-discount content (last-visited-song prompts, practice-mode upsells, singing feature, course recommendations, feature videos), opening the door to personalization and in-app CRM integration and to synergy with future personalization of the whole discovery feed. In the first iteration the cards act simply as buttons opening a single screen each.

## Pre-launch research & rationale
- **References.** The team collected competitor references (music-learning and consumer-subscription apps) using a promo showcase alongside or instead of a single promo tile — one "same as our idea", one a variation pointing toward a more personalized block.
- **Content scoping (Q&A).** The first viewport already shows both the discount tile and the Spotify feature tile; these are combined into the top two positions of the new showcase. Two more cards are added for the first iteration — a course-recommendation card and a tuner card — partly to measure carousel throughput and per-position conversion. Stories-style content was noted as a potential later option.
- **Reach & impact model (internal data, avg daily, Sep–Oct 2025).** The model assumes click→access and access→charge conversion stay flat while view→click improves with the richer format. Baselines: ~8.7k exposed users/day on Android and ~9.8k on iOS; the discount tile converts ~1.2–1.5% of viewers to a click, the Spotify tile ~8–8.8%. Modeled targets: raise discount-tile view→click to 3.0% (Android) / 2.5% (iOS) and Spotify view→click to 10% on both platforms, yielding roughly +$551/day (Android) and +$515/day (iOS) — the model was built to reach a target of at least +$500/day per platform, and works out to an ARPU uplift of ≈65% on the modeled sources ($0.09 → $0.15).

## Hypothesis
If the dynamic multi-offer showcase is implemented for users on the discovery screen, ARPU is expected to increase by 65%, because additional personalization and offer/promo variability should attract more users through the showcase.

## Mechanics
- **Placement:** top of the app's main content-discovery screen, replacing both the static discount tile and the standalone Spotify feature tile (in treatment arms the Spotify offer exists only inside the showcase).
- **Trigger:** shown passively as part of the discovery screen layout; exposure is counted at the analytics event fired when the user dismisses the onboarding banner at the end of the install tour.
- **Timing / Frequency:** persistent surface (no frequency cap); the carousel pages strictly one card at a time, always stopping on a full card, snapping to the nearest card after a swipe, with cyclical scrolling (after the last card, the first is shown again). One arm adds automatic advancement every 8 seconds. If a user buys premium access during the experiment, they are switched back to the control layout of the discovery screen.
- **Audience segment:** new users only — those who complete the install tour during the experiment — and only users finishing the tour without premium rights.
- **Platforms:** iOS and Android.
- **Offer-SKU structure:** no price or product changes — the showcase re-packages existing surfaces as four cards: (1) the seasonal discount offer (same splash-screen logic as before after tap), (2) the Spotify-connection offer (standard connection funnel after purchase), (3) course recommendations (opens the courses section filtered to strumming), (4) the chromatic tuner (opens the tuner, a free tool).

## Experiment design
- **Arms (3):** A — control, current static discount tile plus separate Spotify tile; B — the four-card swipeable showcase with manual scrolling only; C — identical to B plus auto-advance of the carousel every 8 seconds.
- **Primary focus:** conversion to access (goal metric), with revenue/ARPU as the modeled outcome; per-card funnel throughput (view → click → access → charge per card) tracked as supporting metrics.
- **Analysis segments:** one Total segment — free users, mobile platforms, exposure at the tour-end banner-dismiss event.
- **Power / MDE:** power 0.8, alpha 0.05. Android: baseline access CR 0.28% (modeled sources), target lift 92%, 7,453 users per arm, ~2 days. iOS: baseline 0.54%, target lift 40%, 19,114 users per arm, ~4 days. Design summary planned around 1 week of runtime.
- **Instrumentation plan:** three new events for the showcase — view, click, and scroll — each carrying the list of cards available to the user, a shown/hidden flag per card, and (for the discount card) the identifier of the specific sale creative; the click event also names the clicked card. All existing downstream events (splash view/close, paywall view, purchase) are retained with the relevant offer name in their value.
