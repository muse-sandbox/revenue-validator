## PROMPT

You are a senior product experimentation validator for Ultimate Guitar's
revenue/monetization team. You receive a PRE-LAUNCH experiment card (context,
research, hypothesis, mechanics, design, power/MDE). The experiment has not
run yet as far as you know. Your job is to assess the idea and its
experimental design, and to make the launch decision better.

You may also receive a block titled KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT containing an evidence
base of completed revenue experiments (source cards with IDs like T1-02,
pattern cards with IDs like P-07, a closeness model L1/L2/L3, and indices).

### Rules of evidence

1. Every claim about a past experiment MUST cite a source ID that exists in
   the KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT (case IDs T*-**, pattern IDs P-**). If you have no
   KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT, you have no access to specific past experiments: do
   not invent any; clearly mark general reasoning as ungrounded assumption.
2. For every analog you use, state its closeness level per the KNOWLEDGE
   CONTEXT's closeness model (L1 direct / L2 partial / L3 weak) and WHY
   (which axes match exactly, which diverge, and what that does to
   transfer). Use the analog-card format from the KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT.
   Every analog card must include a non-empty not_transferable section.
3. Only the SIGN and MECHANISM of a past effect transfer, and only at L1
   (L2 = hypothesis/warning). Effect magnitudes never transfer as
   predictions; they may be used only as explicitly-labelled sizing priors.
   From L3 evidence transfer ONLY sizing, measurement and guardrail lessons
   — never product conclusions. Transferring a product-level magnitude or
   conclusion from a far analog is a violation.
4. If no L1/L2 analog exists, say "no direct analogs" explicitly. Do not
   promote weak evidence to analog status.
5. Evidence from cases marked inconclusive (or bug-contaminated) grounds
   measurement lessons only.
6. Do not recommend blocking/deprioritizing the hypothesis unless you state
   concrete, evidence-backed reasons; unverified fears are to be phrased as
   risks to instrument, not as blockers.
7. Do not use any tools, search, or external knowledge about the outcomes of
   the specific experiment under review. Judge only from the card (and
   KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT if present).

### Output format (be concise; the whole answer must be reviewable in under
10 minutes; hard cap ~700 words excluding analog cards)

1. **Verdict** — one of: launch as designed / launch with changes /
   redesign before launch / deprioritize. One-sentence rationale.
2. **Predicted outcome** — expected direction of the primary metric with
   uncertainty; state what would surprise you.
3. **Top risks & failure modes** — max 5 bullets, each with the mechanism
   and, when grounded, a source ID.
4. **Analogs** (only if KNOWLEDGE CONTEXT present) — analog cards per the
   format, ranked by closeness; declare conflicts between analogs
   explicitly instead of averaging.
5. **Design & measurement checklist** — concrete fixes: does the goal
   metric match the touched surface/segment; delivery/exposure gates;
   SRM/activation; maturity horizon for trial windows; guardrails and
   stop-rules worth adding.
6. **Changes that would most improve expected value** — max 3, actionable.

# RH-01 — Ad-slot re-trial promo splash for users with expired subscriptions

## Product context
The Ultimate Guitar mobile app shows full-screen ad interstitials to ad-eligible users on tab opens, capped at 5 impressions per day with a 180-second minimum interval. Users whose paid subscription has ended lose their premium rights and fall back into this ad-monetized pool. The team had already run experiments that replace the ad in this slot with the app's own promotional content (a paywall shown after the ad slot, and a promotional video instead of the ad), treating the ad slot as an owned, high-reach delivery channel. This experiment applies that delivery channel to a specific audience: users with a previously active but currently inactive subscription, who are deliberately offered a personalized promotional offer — including the possibility of a renewed free trial — to bring them back to a paid plan.

## Pre-launch research & rationale
The pre-launch work on the page covered five strands:

1. **Prior reactivation attempts (described generically, with their outcomes).**
   - A "scary" non-removable banner on the Explore screen plus a splash after auto-renewal was turned off: only ~30–32% of Explore openers actually saw the banner (data flagged for re-verification); banner view→click was very low (iOS 6.1%/7.2%, Android 25.8%/13.2% in the two treatment arms); click→purchase 9–14% (iOS) and 37–40% (Android); total view→charge ~0.2–1.1% iOS, 0–2.6% Android; iOS ARPU by arm $0.45/$0.62/$0.69, Android $0.40/$0.56/$0.30. Hypothesis not confirmed — small reach and weak click-through despite a small ARPU increase.
   - A push notification plus personalized splash right after auto-renewal cancel (iOS): the "extra trial" arm (30-day trial) was best on conversions and revenue (ARPU $1.04 vs $0.81 control; splash→click 5.4%; click→access 58.46%), but only ~50% of sent pushes were delivered, splash reach was under 80% of the target group, and up to 20% never saw the splash — goal missed due to poor push delivery and incorrect display accounting.
   - A repeat of that cancel-moment experiment with a 6-month trial (iOS+Android): pushes 1,358 sent / 988 delivered (72%); 831 opened the app after cancel (29.5% vs 30.2% control); 703 saw the splash (25%); splash→click 3.84% (27 clicks); splash→purchase 1.28% (9 purchases); ARPU $0.31→$0.37 (+22%, p=0.54); ARPPU $25.38→$27.06 (+6.6%, p=0.36); revenue $837.7→$1,055.3 (+26%); re-subscription 1.21%→1.38% (+14%, not significant). Conclusion: a six-month trial alone was not a strong enough incentive — price generosity alone doesn't tempt; intangible/personalized bonuses were suggested.
2. **An accidental discovery from an earlier ad-slot offer experiment.** In a previous project that put a promotional offer into the ad slot, a segmentation error let users with expired subscriptions into the experiment and offered them an additional trial. Although these users had already used their trial and were charged immediately, they demonstrated a high conversion rate. This project deliberately targets that audience and designs a special promotional offer that allows them to start a new trial.
3. **Ad-pressure research.** External sources reviewed on the page suggest 7–9 impressions/day is ~7–9% of a user's total daily ad noise — noticeable but not dominating, enough discomfort to motivate subscribing (especially with a splash explaining that premium removes ads), above the current 5-impression cap but below the scare-away 10–12 zone; conversion-focused campaigns can run higher frequency than image campaigns (2–3/day), with testing advised to avoid burnout.
4. **Audience behavior data (internal).** On average 140 users whose subscription ended that day return to the app and open at least one tab, seeing ~70 ads/day as a group; the number declines only ~10% over two weeks (day 14: 126 returning, 50 seeing ads). Ad exposure is heavily skewed to zero: 77.7% see 0–1 ads/day (51.4% none, 26.3% one; 625/319/134 users at 0/1/2 impressions), and only 24 of 1,215 (~2%) hit the 5/day cap. About 20% open only one tab/day (303 users at 1 tab, 214 at 2, down to 38 at 14 tabs/day); the 5-impression cap and 180s interval only affect the <10% who open 6+ tabs. Recorded assessments: raising the cap 5→8 would affect only 1–2% of the audience ("for 98% of users, the increase in the limit will have no effect"); shortening the 180s interval helps only the "hardcore" ~5–7% (<100 people); a splash on EVERY tab open would reach ~1,500 users and at an optimistic 1% CR yield ~$600/day ($480/day counting from the 2nd tab) — judged very optimistic, with a risk of scaring away loyal users.

## Hypothesis
As stated pre-launch (paraphrased): if a unique promotional offer, including a renewed trial, is presented specifically to users with a previously active but currently inactive subscription via full-screen in-app splashes in the ad slot, ARPU and repurchase rate in this audience are expected to increase — without negatively impacting 7-day retention or engagement (users with 3+ tabs/scores weekly). The significance level was left as a template placeholder ("XX%") in the document.

## Mechanics
- **Placement:** a full-screen splash with the unique offer appears instead of an ad interstitial on the tab surface. When the splash is displayed automatically on tab entry, a pre-splash screen is shown first; when the user reaches it by clicking a banner, only the splash is shown. Banner entry points also exist on the tab page, song results page, and search page (per the instrumentation's source values).
- **Trigger:** the first time the user opens a tab each day, the splash takes the ad's place; on subsequent tab opens that day, regular ads run as usual. Additionally, if a regular ad fails to load or errors, the promotional splash is shown instead (fallback).
- **Timing / Frequency:** once per day (first tab open), resetting daily; the offer itself can be used at most once — after purchasing through it, the splash no longer appears for that user.
- **Audience segment:** only users with a previously active but currently inactive subscription.
- **Platforms:** shipped on iOS ("The experiment will be available on iOS platform"); Android was sized in the design model only.
- **Offer-SKU structure:** subscription `com.ultimateguitar.tabs.plus.inst.1year2` with a `14DAYSFREE` promotion offer — 2 weeks of additional free trial, granted even if the user previously had a trial.

## Experiment design
- **Arms (2):** control — normal ads; variation 2 — the offer splash per the mechanics above.
- **Primary focus:** goal metric "eligible user → access, %" (conversion of the targeted segment to subscription access); the model also projects ARPU gains (iOS +$0.042, ~+6%; Android +$0.06, ~+14%). Guardrails per the hypothesis: 7-day retention and engagement (3+ tabs/scores weekly); ad revenue displacement is implicitly at stake since the splash consumes the first ad slot of the day.
- **Analysis segments (calc config):** Total {'pro_rights': 'all'}; "Without offer-sourced accesses" (funnel_source_exclude of the new splash source value); "Offer-sourced accesses only" (funnel_source_include, for conversion research purposes); plus a funnel segment: members > Splash View > [Splash Close; Banner Purchase Click > Purchase Process Finish], all filtered to the new splash source value.
