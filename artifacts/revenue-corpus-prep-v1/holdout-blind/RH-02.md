# RH-02 — Once-daily subscription splash replacing third-party commercial full-screens for free users

## Product context
The Ultimate Guitar app shows free users third-party ad units: full-screen interstitials on the tab surface and banner slots elsewhere. Alongside them exist "zero states" — placements where an ad fails to load or nothing is served — which historically earned nothing. This project belongs to a program whose stated goal is "to earn additional revenue with changing the zero states ads for selling subscription on the app": redesigning the existing ad-source real estate so that some of it sells UG subscriptions directly instead of (or in addition to) serving ads. The tested surface is the app's own full-screen subscription splash (leading into a pre-paywall and then the main paywall) plus subscription-offer banners, deployed in the slots normally occupied by ads and ad zero-states, on both mobile platforms.

## Pre-launch research & rationale
The team explicitly framed this as a combination of learnings from three earlier projects on the same layer (described generically):

1. An early test that swapped an interstitial into a landing-style screen "got pretty poor results, yet we saw the perspectives of its kind of source" — the surface looked promising despite the weak first attempt.
2. A very similar direct predecessor project (a prior iteration line of this same idea) "resulted in an increase in ARPU, but we decided not to roll it out and to experiment more with the format".
3. A test that for the first time displayed the app's own full-screen unit instead of advertising once per day: this mechanic "significantly increased the reach of the experiment".

The pitch concludes: "Now we want to repeat and combine the experience from previous experiments" — i.e., pair the high-reach once-per-day replacement trigger from (3) with the subscription-selling creative direction from (2), while also covering ad-failure zero states as in earlier tests.

## Hypothesis
If the app's own subscription paywall is shown to all free users once per day in place of the usual third-party full-screen ad, conversion to paid subscriptions and overall revenue will increase by at least XX% (the expected effect size was left as a placeholder in the document).

## Mechanics
- **Placement:** three coordinated placements — (a) the full-screen interstitial slot on the tab surface, showing the app's own splash instead of the regular ad; (b) the same splash whenever an ad fails to load, as in previous experiments; (c) banners with subscription offers in all zero-ad states and as replacements for ad banners.
- **Trigger:** the daily splash fires on the user's first eligible tab open of the day (the same trigger as the earlier once-per-day video-replacement experiment); the ad-fail splash fires on load failure.
- **Timing / Frequency:** the splash is shown once per day per user (plus ad-fail occurrences); its close button appears only after 5 seconds.
- **Audience segment:** only users without any subscription (fully free users).
- **Platforms:** iOS and Android.
- **Offer-SKU structure:** the splash leads to a pre-paywall screen and then the main paywall; tapping a subscription banner deep-links straight to the paywall, skipping the pre-paywall. The offer in this first iteration is a 14-day free trial of the premium tier (analytics source values are named around the 14-day-free framing; the calculation plan includes a segment defined by trial length = 14).

## Experiment design
- **Arms (2):** control — regular ads and zero states as-is; variation — the daily/ad-fail subscription splash plus subscription banners as described above.
- **Primary focus:** ARPU is the goal metric. Downstream subscription conversion (access and charge), trial share and trial-to-paid quality, retention, tab-view engagement, and ad revenue displacement are tracked around it.
- **Analysis segments:** total free users; a cannibalization-check segment excluding subscriptions attributed to this surface's own source values; a long-trial segment (trial = 14 days); ex-subscriber splits (expired-subscription users, with and without this surface's attributed accesses); and a step-by-step splash → banner view → purchase-click → purchase-finish funnel over the new source values.
- **Power / MDE:** design table (audience labeled as users who failed to view an ad interstitial; parameters inherited from the earlier daily-video test's design; seasonality "took 1 week in calculations"):

| Parameter | iOS | Android |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline ARPU | $0.127 | $0.056 |
| Lift, % | 23.15% | 12.89% |
| MDE | 0.017 | 0.10 |
| Power | 0.8 | 0.8 |
| Alpha | 0.05 | 0.05 |
| Sample size (per variation) | 109,556 | 293,260 |
| Duration (days) | 12 | 8 |

- **Instrumentation plan:** an experiment-exposure event sent for both variations; splash view/close events and the existing banner purchase funnel (banner upgrade view, purchase click, purchase process start/finish/canceled) extended with new source values distinguishing the interstitial, tab, song, and search placements of the 14-day-free offer.

Open program questions carried into this launch: whether broad daily exposure of every free user (rather than only ad-failure cases) is the right frequency, and whether the 5-second-delayed close affects perceived UX quality.
