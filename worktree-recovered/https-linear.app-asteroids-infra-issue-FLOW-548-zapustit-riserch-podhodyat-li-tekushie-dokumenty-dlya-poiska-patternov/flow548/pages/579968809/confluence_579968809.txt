# Research and Context

Goal: Generate additional revenue through sales options in the profile section

Foundation : Moving subscription information up to the profile level can give more visibility to subscription plans, making it easier for users to view and access available options. This change may also simplify navigation and reduce the steps needed for users interested in upgrading or renewing their subscription, ultimately boosting conversion rates

### Research results

Right now, the profile consists of two nested levels:

Profile

Settings

Currently, subscription information is hidden within the settings, so it’s not visible upon opening the profile. In this experiment, we’ll try moving the information about plans directly into the profile to make it visible to a larger audience.

— we also previously conducted an experiment with an offboarding process, where we tested moving subscription plans into the profile for a limited audience. This change resulted in an additional X (tbd ) accesses per day.

## Decision

Results

No significant changes in terms of revenue for both variations. 

The 3rd group (bundle) looks even worse in terms of the main metrics, the 2nd group (only Pro) in general does not differ significantly from the test group.

Conclusion 

Despite some minor successes on Android, the target metrics were not achieved

B : a little higher 1d retention (only for iOS), but not significantly (probably by chance). for those who have selected a tariff plan on the account page : a little more accesses to a Pro plan through iOS, but less to Courses and bundles (Pro + Courses + Sing). Almost no changes for Android users.

C : Expectedly more accesses to bundle (Pro + Courses + Sing), however conversions (trial, charges) are much lower, therefore ARPU is lower (significantly for iOS).  

After launching the experiment, the app’s interface and navigation changed: profile access is now at the top of the page and less noticeable. As a result, the collected data can no longer be considered fully relevant

Nevertheless, the profile from the first iteration has been rolled out and will remain in the same format

The feature comparison element can be reused in other places, such as on Explore instead of a banner

We won’t continue developing the experiment in this form, but we’re closing it as successful since we rolled out the first variation
