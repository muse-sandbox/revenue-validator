Research and Context

  • Goal: Reduce unsubscribes and motivate users to renew their subscriptions by reminding them of the benefits they will lose. In doing so, reach as many users as possible, including trial users
  • Foundation: Implement a “scary” banner on the Explore screen that appears after users turn off auto-renewal subscriptions. This banner will emphasize the potential loss of benefits and offer the option to renew the subscription. In addition to the scare banner, we'll add a splash that will appear for the user when they log into the app when they don't have an auto-renewal subscription. 

Research results

  • Currently, users can turn off auto-renewal for their subscriptions through the app store, and there are no in-app mechanisms to remind them of the benefits they might lose. Our hypothesis is that by introducing a "scary" banner directly on the Explore screen, right after users disable auto-renewal, we can effectively communicate the consequences of their decision. The banner will highlight what users will miss out on once their subscription expires and offer a seamless way to renew.
  • We already had a similar project [2024-08-08-08] UG App - non-removable subscription expiration banner [2024-XX-XX], it had a problem with the number of affected users
  • This time we want to increase the number of engaged users by including trial users

Hypothesis

If we implement a reminder banner as well as a splash when opening the app for users with auto-renewal disabled and an active trial period in the Explore screen scenario, we expect the renewal rate to increase by an additional 9% without decreasing overall user engagement, as the banner will emphasize the unique benefits of the subscription and provide a clear call to action. The significance level is set at 5%.

Description of the Solution & Mockups

Solution

  • The experiment works for users with a trial subscription with auto-renewal disabled and less than 2 weeks to the end of the subscription
  • Only users who have subscriptions from the Subscriptions table below (current subscription) participate in the experiment
  • For the described group of users, when logging into the app, a splash appears with a description of the app's benefits and an indication that the subscription is about to expire
    • Splash only appears once for a particular type of expiring subscription. If a user's pro subscription is expiring, they will only see a splash once. We need to store locally information about the splashes seen
  • In addition to the splash, the user gets an unremovable banner
    • This banner can be hidden by clicking on the “close” button, but it will reappear in the next session
    • The banner will only disappear after user purchase the subscription it offers
    • Or if the current subscription expires and the user ceases to be premium
  • ABC variations are assumed in the experiment
    • A - Control variation that does not include any changes
    • B - A variation in which we offer the user their current subscription
    • С - A variation in which we offer the user a discounted subscription, relative to the one he already has
      • A list of subscripts for Variation C is listed below. There is a current subscription and a subscription that we offer to the user depending on the current subscription

Decision

RedFAIL

  • The conversion rate for an offer with a paywall is 1% or lower on iOS and 2-6% on Android. When we do this conversion, we don't get the desired absolute values for additional revenue.
  • The banner on Explore performs 5 times better on iOS (CTR 30%) compared to 7-8% on Android
