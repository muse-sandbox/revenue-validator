# Pitch

## Initial Context & Idea

Goal: increase net revenue per web paywall visitor by collapsing the plan lineup to a single clear trial + monthly choice and repositioning the seasonal annual offer as a higher-priced save offer at funnel exit

Foundation : 

the current web paywall presents three plans simultaneously: annual with trial, discounted instant annual (no trial), and monthly (no trial)

users are required to evaluate multiple annual options before making a purchase decision, increasing cognitive load and comparison friction, the discounted annual offer currently competes directly with the primary annual trial offer, potentially cannibalizing higher-value conversions

will likely be a shift toward more expensive annual trials

presenting a targeted annual discount only at the moment of abandonment may improve recovery without affecting the main purchase path

the first iteration should be created only for the permanent banner, to test the concept on a single platform

## Decision 
[macro]
Red fail
[/macro]

Do not roll out

Both the Total-segment monetization metrics and the permanent banner funnel move the same negative direction for the test: ARPU -10.4%, ARPPU -4.2%, trial → charge -9.2%, offset only by +8.0% more trials. On the permanent banner specifically, paywall → subscribed conversion drops 40% relative (17.35% → 10.42%), cutting channel net revenue by 31% ($2,152 → $1,479). Groups are balanced, and the design (6 days, sample size) was met as planned. Churn 14d / refund 14d are preliminary — ~65% of charges are younger than the 14-day observation window (experiment ended 2026-06-25, window closes ~2026-07-09)

The "fewer plans → less doubt → higher conversion" hypothesis is not supported by this test

Permanent banner funnel:

Top of funnel is better for the test: banner click +19% relative (0.72% → 0.86%), click → paywall view +12% relative (13.07% → 14.68%) — the new banner (without the seasonal instant offer) is more attractive and drives more people to the paywall.

But paywall → subscribed drops 40% relative (17.35% → 10.42%) — collapsing to 2 plans (trial + monthly) sharply hurts paywall conversion, outweighing the top-of-funnel gain.

Net effect on this channel: net revenue -31% ($2,152 → $1,479). This is only part of total revenue (PermanentBanner is ~10% and ~7% of total revenue for A/B respectively), but the direction matches the Total-segment metrics — a consistent signal, not an isolated fluke.

Takeaway: the hypothesis "fewer plans → less doubt → higher conversion" is not supported — the opposite happens: more traffic reaches the paywall, but choosing between 2 plans converts worse than choosing between 3. The removed instant offer likely worked as an anchor/alternative for part of the audience, and without it some users simply do not subscribe rather than moving to trial.

### Next steps

Keep the permanent banner concept — it drives meaningfully more clicks and paywall views (CTR +19% relative) — but revert the paywall back to 3 plans instead of 2
