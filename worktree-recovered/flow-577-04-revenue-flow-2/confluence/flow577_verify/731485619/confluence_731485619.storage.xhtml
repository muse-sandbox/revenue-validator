Pitch

Context & Idea

Goal

We want to improve our offering scenarios before the start of the sales season and enhance conversion and revenue

Foundation

Currently, we have a seasonal promotional campaign in our App that includes a thematic splash screen and banner on the Explore screen with seasonal visuals. It's a "permanent" sale where the offer doesn't change; we only update the theme for the current season. The scenario itself is fine, but it doesn't look very compelling and doesn't leverage additional marketing opportunities beyond the direct discount.

Example for Autumn sale

Explore BannerSplash Screen

Assets

Idea

If we develop more emotionally engaging scenarios and visuals for a seasonal campaign (including animation, sounds, and haptic feedback), we can boost revenue by making our product look more professional and valuable, which will drive more emotional purchases. We also expect that emotional design can maintain our conversion rates even if we reduce discount sizes.

We can test this approach during the Halloween sale, and if successful, apply it to other sale seasons.

Decision RedFAIL

  • Based on early results and increasing conversion in the sales funnel, we rolled out the experimental group to everyone (period - )
  • However, the final results showed that due to cannibalization of other sources by the sale source, we ended up with less revenue despite having more paying users. Ultimately, we cannot use this mechanic permanently. Potentially, this mechanic could work to boost major one-time sales events with organic traffic growth (Black Friday, New Year, Christmas)
  • Also, mechanics with emotional design can be tested and integrated into our regular scenarios to increase conversion

  1. Overall, there are no statistically significant differences between the main monetization and retention metrics.

    • The only cases were CR to Tab View 300s and 600s for the Android platform, but since CR to Tab View is the same => it is difficult to say that the cause lies in the current experiment.
  2. Despite the lack of statistical significance, It’s worth noting that:

    • CR to Access is higher in the test group (+6.8% on iOS and +3.3% on Android),

    • However, ARPU in this group is slightly lower — by approximately -2% on both platforms. It can be explained:

      • By cannibalization;

      • By lower CR from Access to Charge.
        • However, this seems to be less a result of the experiment and more a result of random distribution, which, due to the insufficient number of observations, shows a difference (though it is still not statistically significant).
  3. When calculating the Halloween Funnel, a statistically significant difference in ARPU on the iOS platform was found in favor of the experimental group.
    • However, there was also a statistically significant difference in CR to Splash View → that is, the fact that users in the experimental group, for some reason, saw the splash more often and could have affected ARPU.

  4. For the Halloween funnel, it was found that users encounter the Banner Splash less frequently (since it appears after clicking on the banner). However, this splash screen generates more subscribers and revenue.

  5. On the iOS platform, the Automatic Splash in the experimental group showed a higher conversion rate from Splash View to Splash Click, but it did not further convert into subscriptions. On the Android platform, the conversion from click to subscription for this splash was also worse in the experimental group.

Conclusion:

  1. When looking at all user purchases in the experiment by source, some cannibalization can be observed among non-sale sources.
    • On iOS, the largest losses in accesses and revenue came from the Official Tabs% sources.
    • On Android, the biggest drops in accesses and revenue also occurred in the Official Tabs% sources, as well as in Smartscroll.
  2. If we check which of those who saw Other Paywall bought it vs. bought Halloween => it's not statistically significant, but it's true that both in absolute numbers and as a share, more users in the test group bought Halloween compared to the control group.
