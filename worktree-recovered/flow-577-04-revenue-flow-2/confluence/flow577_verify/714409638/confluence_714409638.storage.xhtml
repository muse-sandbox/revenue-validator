Research and Context

  • Goal: Increase the conversion of former subscribers back to paid plans in the Ultimate Guitar mobile app by offering them a personalized promotional offer, including the possibility of a renewed trial, through targeted interstitials.

  • Foundation: Former subscribers have previously demonstrated a willingness to pay and showed high conversion rates when offered a renewed trial by mistake in an earlier experiment. In this project, we will intentionally target this segment with a clear, attractive offer to encourage their return to a paid subscription.

Research results

  • We have already conducted experiments with our own advertising instead of interstitials, which have already proven themselves well [2025-07-07] UG iOS: paywall – paywall after ad interstitial [2025-XX-XX]

  • We also conducted experiments with our promotional video instead of interstitials, and the results show that there is potential here as well [2025-07-09] UG App: paywall – monetization video instead of ad interstitials [2025-XX-XX]

  • We've launched several experiments with winback offers, based on the main assumption that the sooner a user sees the winback offer after canceling, the more likely they are to accept it. This way, we hope to bring more users back to the subscription.

    • [2025-02-17] UG App: churn – winback right after cancel [2025-XX-XX]

      [2025-02-17] UG App: churn – winback right after cancel [2025-XX-XX]

      Goals: 

      • A repeat attempt to adjust the previous similar “win-back” experiment: create a mechanism to bring back iOS and Android users who canceled their Pro+, Pro+Courses+Sing auto-renewal subscriptions - immediately after canceling, send them a push notification and show a splash screen with a time-limited discount offer to bring back at least 5% of those who canceled and increase ARPU.

To really increase return and retention, it is necessary to supplement the price offer with intangible and personalized bonuses - exclusive content and early access, mentoring support or an individual plan, gamification elements, social and affiliate features, short video notifications.

How many ads can we show a user to motivate them to upgrade to pro?
  • This article reviews the total amount of advertising that users see per day. Based on this data, a frequency of 7–9 impressions on Ultimate Guitar is ~7–9% of the total noise, which is enough to be noticeable but not excessive.
    • A frequency of 7–9 creates discomfort that motivates users to subscribe, especially with a splash screen that explains how to remove ads. It is a noticeable share, but it does not dominate the perception.
  • This article states that for campaigns focused on conversion (in our case, upgrading to premium), the frequency can be higher than for image-building purposes (2–3 impressions per day). However, the article emphasizes the importance of testing to avoid “burning out” the audience.
    • A frequency of 7–9 impressions per day creates enough pressure for the ad to become noticeable and motivate users to subscribe, especially with the addition of a splash screen explaining that the premium version removes ads. This is higher than the current 5 impressions, which is in line with our hypothesis of increasing ad load, but does not reach the extreme 10–12 impressions that could scare users away.

  • Among those who open many tabs per day (e.g., 7+), the absolute number of users drops rapidly (e.g., 38 people open 14 tabs per day).

Does it make sense to reduce the interval between advertisements?
  • For most of the audience, again, no.
    • The 180-second limit restricts ad display only to frequent users who open 6+ tabs per day.
    • The vast majority of users simply do not fall under either the limit or the interval — they do not even reach 5 impressions.
    • Reducing the interval will result in a negligible increase only in the “hardcore” group (≈5–7% of the audience, according to pessimistic estimates in absolute terms — no more than 100 people)
Does it make sense to increase the ad display limit from 5 to 8 per day?
  • There is no significant meaning for the majority of users.
    • According to the data, only 24 users out of 1,215 (≈2%) see the maximum allowed 5 ads per day (or per week, if the data is aggregated).
    • The vast majority (77.7%) see either 0 or 1 ad per period (51.4% see none at all, 26.3% see only 1 ad).
    • The increase in the limit to 8 will only affect those who open a lot of content — no more than 1–2% of the audience (approximately 24–41 people out of 1,215).
    • For 98% of users, the increase in the limit will have no effect.
Does it make sense to show a splash screen every time the tab is opened?
  • 1,500 users will see this splash screen, and with a conversion rate of 1%, that amounts to around $600 per day (4,200 per week).
  • If, for example, we take the conversion rate from the second tab only (i.e., viewing the splash screen), there are already 1,200 users, and if 1% convert, that will be around $480 per day (3,360 per week).
  • This is a very optimistic scenario; most likely, the conversion rate will be less than 1%, and we may scare away loyal users. The potential conversion rate looks low.
Similar experience with previous advertising experiments

Previously, we launched a similar project: [2025-04-11] UG App: paywall – offer instead of ad interstitials [2025-07-10]. Due to a segmentation error, former subscribers were included in the experiment and offered an additional trial. Although these users had already used their trial and were charged immediately, they demonstrated a high conversion rate.

With this new project, we are deliberately targeting former subscribers and designing a special promotional offer that allows them to start a new trial and potentially return.

Description of the Solution & Mockups

Solution

  • The experiment will be available on iOS platform.
  • Only users with previously active, but currently inactive, subscriptions will participate.
  • Our splash screen with a unique offer will appear instead of an ad the first time a user opens a tab each day.
    • On subsequent tab openings within the same day, users will see regular ads as usual.
    • The next day, the offer will again be shown once on their first tab opening.
    • However, if a regular ad fails to load or an error occurs, our promotional splash screen will be shown instead.
  • We will use a promotion offer that will allow the user to get 2 weeks of additional trial, even if they previously had a trial.
    • We suggest that such users purchase a subscription com.ultimateguitar.tabs.plus.inst.1year2 with a 14DAYSFREE promotion offer.
  • If the interstitial is displayed automatically when entering the tab, the pre-splash is displayed.
    • If the user clicks on the banner, only the splash screen is displayed.
  • The user cannot take advantage of this offer more than once - after purchasing this subscription, this interstitial will no longer appear.

Decision

Results 

  • Monetization — [UGT_IOS / Total / Variation 2]

    • Revenue metrics (ARPU): +42.8%, increase in revenue per user

    • Funnel entry metrics (access CR, charge CR, trial share): +170.9%, +37.8%, +293.8% respectively, strong lift in entering and progressing the purchase flow

    • Early churn (charge -> 14d cancel, charge -> 1m cancel): +117.5% and +69.0%, higher post‑charge cancellations

    • Tab view metrics

      • Engagement per user (tab views per user; 60s/120s/180s per user): +18.5% and +2.9%–3.4%, higher consumption

      • tab view 60s, %: −0.67%, slight drop in the share of users viewing ≥60s 

Conclusion GreenSUCCESS

  • Can roll out test variation
  • On iOS, the promo splash with a 14‑day re‑trial for previously active but currently inactive subscribers meaningfully increases monetization and engagement:
    • ARPU is up ~43%, access and charge conversion rates are higher, and users consume more tabs.
    • The effect is concentrated among users who actually see the interstitial; those without interstitial access do not move.
    • However, early cancellations jump sharply (+69% to +118%). This pattern is consistent with the offer attracting many low‑intent re‑trialists who cancel quickly. Retention is unchanged.
