Pitch

Initial Context & Idea

  • Goal: increase the number of successful paid accesses on WEB by lowering entry price barriers to improve conversion from visitors to payers and enlarge total revenue 
  • Foundation: 

    • сurrent pricing creates a high entry barrier for new and low-intent users

    • by lowering prices, we will attract more customers and expand our customer base

    • more Pro+ subscriptions will result in more upsells, which will generate additional revenue

Confidence research

In 2021, we raised our prices, which led to a significant increase in revenue. 

Previous сhange of plans:

Now that upsells have been introduced, and in order to grow our subscriber base, we want to bring back lower prices.

Reach & Impact

    3. the conversion rate from subscribe to charge for the trial plans $39.99 will be 36%

Results

#7268  – 

Exposure event: Landing Plans View

Decision GreenSuccess

  • Revenue up 3.2% immediately and up to 6.74% over a 3-year horizon due to the cumulative effect of retention
  • The main drivers were Instant plans (+130–160%) and upsells (bundles +38%, courses/books +27%); these offset the negative impact of monthly and trial subscriptions
  • Engagement metrics confirm that the product is actually being consumed by a new audience
  • Comeback offer -20% and conversion to charge at exchange rates/book rates -20%
    • perhaps some additional optimization is needed here.
  • We consider the experiment a success

Next step

  • Rolling out lower prices, but we are also implementing a monitoring system to track whether the price reduction is being offset by the anticipated growth and whether the actual trends align with the forecast model

What is the forecast?

  • Access count grew by 65%, charges by 87.5%, and final actual revenue by 4% (or +$3,545).

  • Among subscriptions, Instant Offers showed the strongest growth — +130-160% vs. Control, which fully offset the price reduction in actual revenue and drove an increase in the forecast.

  • Monthly subscriptions grew by 83%, but this wasn't enough to offset the price drop, so both actual and forecast revenue are lower in the test compared to Control.

  • For trial subscriptions, growth was even lower — around 29% — but thanks to a higher trial-to-charge conversion, total charges grew by 89%. However, this still wasn't enough to compensate for the price reduction, so both actual and forecast revenue for these subscriptions are below Control.

  • Also the decrease in Comeback offer is observed by 20% for both – accesses and revenue.

  • Upsells grew disproportionately to the core subscriptions:

    • Bundle subscriptions: +38%

    • Courses/books: +27% (with conversion to charge dropping by almost 20% for courses/books)

  • Overall, Instant subscriptions and upsell growth were the main drivers. This resulted in forecast revenue of +3% in year 1, +4.9% in year 2, and +7.75% in year 3

  • Post-rollout, the 14-day cancel rate for Pro subscriptions dropped from ≈13% to 9–10%. Notably, this runs counter to the experiment itself, where the test group actually showed a higher cancel rate for Pro than control (11.3% vs 10.7%).
  • For Non-Pro, cancel rate remains at ≈27–28% (up since the experiment launched on April 9). Important caveat: a separate double-upsell experiment was running over the same period, which raised cancel rate in its own test arm — this likely explains part of the Non-Pro increase, independent of the pricing change.
  • Combined (Pro + Non-Pro), cancel rate post-rollout has dropped to ≈11–12%, down from ≈13–15%.

Revenue check

Comparing daily averages of charges, charges − cancels, charged revenue minus refunded revenue, and AOV across two periods — Apr 30–May 19 (pre-rollout) vs May 21–Jun 14 (post-rollout):

  • AOV declined by -44%, closely matching the experiment result (-44.6%) — the price effect reproduced almost exactly.
  • Charges grew by +77.5%, notably below the growth observed in the experiment (+88.9%). This gap is the main reason full-rollout revenue came in essentially flat (-0.45%), whereas the experiment showed a revenue increase driven by stronger growth in accesses.

In other words, subscription volume roughly doubled, but the resulting revenue uplift is comparatively modest — a reminder that the per-subscriber value of the cheaper cohort is meaningfully lower, so raw volume growth doesn't translate one-to-one into revenue growth.

Bottom line

The drop in cancel rate is a positive leading indicator for retention, and the two-year cohort projection confirms the direction is positive rather than negative — full-rollout revenue is not at risk. However, the magnitude of the upside is modest (+1.5% over two years) and smaller than the experiment implied, given how much cheaper the AOV is relative to the volume gained. At this stage, the retention improvement should be treated as a real but limited offset, not a guarantee that revenue growth will meaningfully outpace the price cut over time.
