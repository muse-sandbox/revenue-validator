# RH-06 — Streamlined two-option plan menu on an always-visible web upgrade surface

## Product context
On the Ultimate Guitar website, free visitors are monetized through a persistent site-wide promotional strip that is always visible at the top of the page and leads into a subscription paywall. At the time of this experiment, that paywall presented three plans simultaneously: an annual plan with a free trial, a discounted instant annual plan with no trial (a seasonal $19.99 offer), and a monthly plan with no trial. The team's framing of the problem: users are required to evaluate multiple annual options before making a purchase decision, which increases cognitive load and comparison friction, and the discounted annual offer competes directly with the primary annual-trial offer, potentially cannibalizing higher-value conversions. The stated goal was to increase net revenue per web paywall visitor by collapsing the plan lineup to a single clear trial + monthly choice and repositioning the seasonal annual discount as a higher-priced save offer shown only at funnel exit. The concept was scoped deliberately narrowly: the first iteration was to be built only for this one always-on surface, to test the idea on a single platform before touching other paywalls.

## Pre-launch research & rationale
The team built a bottom-up channel model from internal web funnel data for 2026-05-21 – 2026-06-05 (with a concurrently running plan-length experiment excluded from the data, plus an "estimation" column reconstructing the full picture as if that experiment had not existed). Baseline (estimated, full picture): ~531,697 strip views, ~3,488 clicks, ~479 plan-menu views, ~98 accesses in the period, with plan-menu view → access at 20.50%. Purchases in the channel decomposed by offer type: the seasonal discounted instant annual accounted for ~32 of ~98 accesses — a large share of the channel — alongside trials (~8), exit-funnel save offers (~23), full-price instants, and monthly.

Modeled assumptions for the test arm:
1. Strip view → click improves +15%, because the new creative is more attractive (it advertises getting full access free).
2. Click → plan-menu view is unchanged.
3. Plan-menu view → access improves +20%, because "less plans is displayed — less doubts what to choose".
4. The removed discounted instant plan's volume largely shifts into trials (modeled trial accesses jumping ~8 → ~56 in the period) and into the repriced exit save offer ($19.99 → $24.99), with remaining instant price points raised toward $39.99.

The model projected channel accesses +38%, charges roughly flat (+1.3%), and charged channel revenue +$501 (+22.2%) over the modeled slice — a deliberate mix shift toward more expensive annual trials at the cost of immediate charges. A known modeling caveat was that access → charge for trials was assumed to improve (24.18% → 30.00%).

## Hypothesis
As stated pre-launch: if, for this always-on surface, the seasonal instant annual is removed from the paywall (leaving only trial + monthly) and the seasonal annual is repositioned as a single, higher-priced exit-intent offer, net revenue is expected to increase by $500 daily, because a two-option choice reduces decision friction and protects trial starts, while moving the discount to an intent-triggered, higher-priced exit offer captures more value per saved user.

## Mechanics
- **Placement:** the paywall reached from the persistent site-wide promotional strip on the web product; the two-plan lineup applies to the premium-tier paywalls in this flow, but the iteration is scoped to this one entry surface only. Only the "white" landing-page variant is kept.
- **Trigger:** user clicks the promotional strip → plan menu; the exit save offer is triggered by abandonment intent (leaving the purchase funnel).
- **Timing / Frequency:** the strip carries a countdown timer; once it expires the timer itself disappears from the strip, but nothing else changes. The strip remains always-on.
- **Audience segment:** web users who saw the promotional strip during the experiment (exposure is conditioned on actually seeing the strip).
- **Platforms:** web only.
- **Offer-SKU structure:** control keeps the three-plan menu (annual with trial, discounted instant annual with no trial, monthly with no trial). Test arm: a reduced menu of exactly two options — the annual trial (selected by default) and the monthly plan; the $19.99 seasonal discounted plan is replaced by a $24.99 seasonal/welcome save offer positioned at funnel exit; the strip creative is changed to promote a discount on the trial offer; the cancellation funnel is refined.

## Experiment design
- **Arms (2):** A — control, current three-plan menu; B — two-plan menu (trial default-selected + monthly) with the seasonal discount moved to a single higher-priced exit offer.
- **Primary focus:** plan-menu view → access conversion as the goal metric; net revenue per visitor as the economic target.
- **Analysis segments:** the full exposed web population, plus the promotional-strip channel funnel (views → clicks → plan-menu views → accesses/charges by offer type).
- **Power / MDE:** baseline 20.50% (plan-menu view → access), MDE 20%, power 0.8, alpha 0.05, sample size 1,602 plan-menu views per variation, duration 6 days, with 1 week of seasonality reflection (design summary: 7 days).
- **Instrumentation plan:** exposure via the standard experiment-start event, conditioned on the user having seen the promotional strip.
