# RH-05 — Condensed web payment form with prominent alternative payment buttons

## Product context
Ultimate Guitar's website sells its premium subscription tiers through web purchase funnels that all terminate on a payment page: a form where the user enters credit-card details, with alternative payment buttons (PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay) available alongside the card form. At the time of this experiment the team observed that on this page the alternative payment buttons did not fit within the viewport — especially on small screens — so a user landing on the page saw the card form but had to scroll to discover the one-click payment options. This experiment redesigns the layout of that payment page for all premium-funnel purchases on desktop web; the products, prices, and offers themselves are untouched.

## Pre-launch research & rationale
The pre-launch rationale was an observational/design argument rather than a modeled one:

1. **The stated problem.** The payment buttons on the payment page do not fit within the viewport. Presumably this affects the number of people who use those buttons and, ultimately, the number of transactions users could potentially make with those payment methods.
2. **The proposed foundation.** Create a compact page design so that all payment buttons are always visible, especially on small screens — improving the payment process and, through it, purchases.
3. **Reach & impact.** The page's Reach & Impact section was left as a template placeholder (an empty "AVG Daily Metrics / A / B / diff" stub); no quantified reach or revenue-impact model was documented pre-launch.

## Hypothesis
As stated pre-launch: if we make the payment page more compact and ensure that all payment buttons are clearly visible, we will increase conversion rates by 3%, because the people who use these buttons will be able to use them.

## Mechanics
- **Placement:** the payment page (card-details form + alternative payment buttons) of all premium-tier purchase funnels on the website.
- **Trigger:** reaching the payment page in the purchase funnel; the change is a persistent layout redesign, not a triggered overlay.
- **Timing / Frequency:** always on — every visit to the payment page during the experiment renders the assigned layout.
- **Audience segment:** everyone entering the payment funnel ("for everyone in the payment funnel"); the configured analysis segment was all rights levels on the Desktop platform.
- **Platforms:** Web (desktop).
- **Offer-SKU structure:** unchanged — same products, prices, and payment methods; only the presentation of the page differs between arms.

## Experiment design
- **Arms (3):** A — control, current payment page. B — condensed layout: the header block is removed, the heading is made smaller, spacing between blocks is reduced; the window height decreases by about fifteen percent. C — the same condensed layout, additionally moving the alternative payment buttons above the card-details form.
- **Primary focus:** purchase conversion on the payment page; the design table's goal metric was documented as "users tab view → subscribed, %".
- **Analysis segments:** a single Total segment — all rights levels, Desktop platform.
- **Power / MDE:** the design table recorded baseline 0.05%, lift 500%, power 0.8, alpha 0.05, sample size 1,996 per variation, computed duration 2 days; the design summary set the planned run at 7 days to reflect weekly seasonality.
- **Instrumentation plan:** the Analytics section's exposure event and exposure conditions were left blank pre-launch; no new events were specified for the redesign (existing funnel/subscription tracking assumed).
