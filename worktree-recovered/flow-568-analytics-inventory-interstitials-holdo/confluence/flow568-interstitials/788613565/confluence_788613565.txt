# Pitch

Reach - Reach of this initiative  directly depends on the specific personalization signal(s)  we choose to use in a given iteration. Different signals (e.g. repeated song, instrument/age group, practice frequency) will naturally cover different shares of the UG App audience on iOS and Android.

Impact - We expect this initiative to primarily impact  revenue / ARPU , not DAU or new traffic, because we are changing the  interstitial monetization layer and its relevance , while traffic volume and acquisition stay the same.

Confidence - Why we believe in this hypothesis:

Past experiments / internal fake doors

The   UG winback interstitial experiments   for former subscribers showed that behavior‑based, locally targeted interstitials can drive around   +42–43% ARPU vs control and up to 10–20× higher conversion  than a generic AD14Free interstitial, which strongly suggests that contextual, segmented messaging converts much better than one‑size‑fits‑all upsells.

Internal analysis of local interstitials indicates that  60–90% of conversions happen within the first 2–6 impressions , after which effectiveness quickly drops, which means that simply repeating a generic interstitial is much less effective than adapting it to user behavior and context

Competitor results / other teams’ experience

RevenueCat’s analysis of   contextual paywalls   shows that paywalls triggered by specific in‑app actions and moments consistently outperform static generic paywalls, because they better align with user intent and motivation at the time of display.

Nami and similar vendors report that   behavior‑driven personalized paywalls   are now a standard lever for increasing subscription conversion and LTV in mobile apps, supporting our direction to move from generic to personalized interstitials.

Optimizely defines   app personalization   as adapting content and messaging to user behavior and context and notes that generic, non‑segmented experiences underperform compared to behavior‑based personalization in engagement and conversion.

Effort - Overall effort is  moderate , because we reuse the existing interstitial and offer infrastructure and mainly add a new personalization layer plus experiment setup.

## Context & Idea

Goal: Increase conversion to Pro+ by introducing  personalized interstitials that adapt message and value proposition to each user’s real behavior (songs, artists, level, time of day), instead of using one generic interstitial for everyone.

Foundation : Today most users see the same generic interstitial, weakly tied to their actual use cases, so we miss high‑intent moments; we want to move to a system of behavioral segments and contextual interstitials (by song, artist, level, time of day) so the message feels “about me” and appears at the right moment, in line with best practices in app personalization and contextual paywalls.

# Description of the Solution & Mockups

## Main idea

We plan to introduce a system of  behavior-based personalized interstitials  in the UG App, where the message and offer are selected not as a single generic upsell, but according to user context, product stage, and monetization eligibility, since contextual targeting and behavioral segmentation are widely used to improve subscription conversion in mobile apps.

Potential signals for this system include:

behavior signals, such as repeatedly opening the same song, frequently viewing beginner tabs, strong preference for a specific artist, or recurring late-night sessions;

user-level signals, such as beginner vs more advanced usage patterns;

offer eligibility signals, such as free user, former paid user, and whether a user has already consumed a specific reactivation offer.

In subsequent iterations, we will test different signals for this approach

## S olution

#7454

We will trigger a new personalized interstitial  when the user opens a song  and becomes eligible for an interstitial impression in that placement.

The interstitial will use the  currently opened song as the contextual anchor  of the message, so the screen feels tied to the user’s immediate intent and not like a generic monetization surface.

In this experiment, we are testing  personalization of the interstitial message and framing , not a new offer model, so commercial logic will remain the same as in the current interstitial setup.

The experiment will work for both  free  and  ex-paid  users, but each segment will receive the product and copy that match its existing monetization eligibility.

Free users will see the same product configuration that is currently used in the standard interstitial flow for free users.

Former subscribers who are eligible for the extra 2-week trial will see the winback version with that same 2-week trial.

## Decision
[macro]
Red Fail
[/macro]

do not roll out iteration #1 as-is. The result is neutral / inconclusive (no significant lift), so iterate before any further investment.

No metric is statistically significant (all p ≥ 0.32); underpowered on the goal metric despite ~80k members/arm — interstitial-attributed conversions are only ~70–165 per arm.

Net conversion is flat: combined per family ≈ control (free 0.155% vs 0.153%; winback 1.59% vs 1.66%).

At impression #1 (~50–60% of all interstitial conversions) the personalized splash converts same-or-worse (free −6%, winback −25%): it wins curiosity but loses intent — paywall→click roughly halves (7.25% → 3.90%).

The one apparent win (winback paywall +4.6×) was an attribution artifact, not real engagement.

No guardrail benefit either; ARPU / churn / refund moves are all within noise.

## Next steps

Align the paywall to the personalized hook — change the paywall / offer, not just the interstitial copy. The biggest leak is paywall→click; an unchanged generic paywall likely creates an expectation mismatch after the personalized promise.

Extend the personalization dose beyond the first impression (it currently reverts to generic after #1, where ~half of conversions live) — test personalized framing on repeat / high-intent impressions.

The next iteration should test offer-level personalization.

One possible direction is to create an additional interstitial for users who stay free for more than a week and offer them a stronger deal that can move them into trial or paid.

Another direction is to target users who previously had a paid subscription, not just a trial, and test a discount instead of another free trial, similar to the current winback logic.

We should continue looking for meaningful user groups and match each group with the offer that is most relevant to their situation.

The main direction is to personalize the offer, not only the message around the offer.

## Insights

Behavioral / funnel deep-dive on top of the standard result tables. Platform: Android only (iOS is excluded — see below). Control = variation 1 (generic interstitial); Test = variation 2 (personalized "song" interstitial). Conversion figures here are event-based ( Purchase Process Finish ), used as a behavioral signal, not net revenue.

The personalized splash did not increase purchase intent. It brought slightly more users to the paywall, but the paywall-to-click conversion dropped almost twice in the free flow: from 7.25% to 3.90%.

This suggests that the personalized screen created curiosity, but the generic paywall after it did not continue the personalized promise. Users reached the payment screen, but were less likely to click the purchase button.

The biggest leak is on the paywall. The splash was more contextual, but the offer and paywall experience stayed generic. As a result, the personalization stopped before the actual purchase decision.

This experiment also suggests that personalization is more likely to work when it changes the offer itself, not only the wrapper around the offer. Previous examples like winback interstitials and discount offers for young users are better examples of meaningful personalization, because the offer matches the user segment.

### Audience & exposure reality

App Experiment Start fires only when a user becomes eligible for an interstitial, which happens only for free / ex-paid users — Pro users never enter. The three configured segments are therefore essentially the same set: Total ≡ Interstitials ≡ Free (v1: 81,189 / 81,189 / 81,187), and Total and Free are identical to the cent in every metric. Group balance is clean (Δ 0.57%).

iOS effectively never ran : real volume exists only for 2026-05-18…05-20 (~14.3k of 14.7k members), then 1–3 users/day. No iOS reading is possible.

Cohort split (correct pro right = last digit of rights ): pure free rights%10=0 ≈ 72.5k/arm; ex-paid rights%10 ∈ {4,5} ≈ 8.3k/arm (~10% of the audience).

### Conversion by impression number (numbered within each interstitial family)

Impressions are counted within each family (free = AD 14Free + AD Song ; winback = AD Winback + AD Winback Song ), so a default AD 14Free shown right after the personalized AD Song counts as the family's 2nd impression, not a fresh #1. ### ⚠️ Winback "paywall lift" is an attribution artifact (not a real effect)

A value-filtered funnel makes the winback paywall rate look like it jumped 6.4% → 29.8% (4.6×) in Test while free barely moved. That asymmetry is a measurement artifact:

By any Banner Upgrade View , all four groups sit at ~66–76% — real paywall reach barely differs.
