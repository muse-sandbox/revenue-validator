# Research and Context

Goal:  the main goal is to earn additional revenue with changing the zero states ads for selling subscription on the app
Foundation:   We want to redesign existing 'zero states' of ad-sources in order to increase sales of UG Subscriptions. Which means we are focusing on selling subscriptions with this existing sources but with the new approach and possible results. 

### Research results

Previously we launched   [2024-04-24] UG iOS: Interstitial - Swap into Landing [2024-08-10]   and got pretty poor results, yet we saw the perspectives of its kind of source. 

We have already launched a very similar experiment, which resulted in an increase in ARPU, but we decided not to roll it out and to experiment more with the format [2025-04-11] UG App: paywall – offer instead of ad interstitials [2025-07-10]

We also conducted an experiment with advertising, where we tried for the first time to apply a mechanism that displays our interstitial instead of advertising once a day. This significantly increased the reach of the experiment. [2025-07-09] UG App: paywall – monetization video instead of ad interstitials [2025-XX-XX]

Now we want to repeat and combine the experience from previous experiments.

# Hypothesis

If we show our subscription paywall to all free users once per day instead of usual interstitial, we will increase conversion to paid subscriptions and overall revenue by at least XX%

# Description of the Solution & Mockups

[macro]

### Solution

Show our own interstitial paywall once per day to all free users , replacing the regular ad interstitial on their first eligible tab open of the day (same trigger as the monetization video experiment ).

Additionally, show the interstitial paywall whenever an ad fails to load , as in previous experiments .

Add banners with subscription offers in all zero-ad states and as replacements for ad banners .

Logic applies only to users without any subscription .

If a user triggers the paywall via the interstitial (daily or adfail), they first see a pre-paywall screen , followed by the main paywall.

If a user clicks a subscription banner, they are sent directly to the paywall , skipping the pre-paywall.

The close button on the interstitial appears after 5 seconds .

## Decision

Results

[UGT_IOS / Total / Variation 2]

ARPU: +10.9%, revenue per user up

AOV & ARPPU: -9.3%–-10.2%, average purchase value down (due to intro plans)

access cr, %: +16.8%, more users reaching monetization

charge cr, %: +24.1%, higher conversion to charge

trial share, %: -9.5%

[UGT_IOS / Total exes / Variation 2]

ARPPU: +8.3%, higher paying-user revenue in this subset

trial -> charge, %: +38.6%, trial conversion up

[UGT_IOS / Without interstitial accesses exes / Variation 2]

ARPPU: +8.8%, higher paying-user revenue in this subset

[UGT_IOS / Total (tab view metrics) / Variation 2]

Tab view 60s/120s/180s, %: -0.64%–-0.87%, slightly shorter on-tab duration share

Tab views per user: +11.5%, content consumption count up

[UGT_ANDROID / Total / Variation 2]

ARPU: +24.6%, strong revenue per user lift

AOV & ARPPU: -12.9%–-14.0%, lower average purchase value

access cr, %: +12.1%, more users hitting monetization

charge cr, %: +45.3%, much higher conversion to charge

trial share, %: -22.0%, fewer trials started

charge -> 14d cancel, %: -26.6%, early cancellations down

[UGT_ANDROID / Total (tab view metrics) / Variation 2]

Tab view 60s, %: -0.54%, slight drop in short-duration share

[UGT_ANDROID / Without interstitial accesses exes / Variation 2]

trial share, %: +159.9%, large increase in trial starts in this subset

subscriber -> buyer, % & subscription -> charge, %: -10.5% and -11.4%, conversion drop in this subset

Conclusion

The paywall interstitial and banners drove clear monetization gains with minimal user-cost signals.

ARPU increased on both iOS (+11.5%) and Android (+25.0%), supported by higher access and charge conversion and stronger downstream conversion (subscriber -> buyer and subscription -> charge).

AOV/ARPPU fell on both platforms, because of the first year intro prices

Android also saw a significant drop in 14‑day cancellations. Retention did not change.

Engagement signals show a small decline in long-view shares (60/120/180s) on both platforms, likely from the 5s-close interstitial friction, but iOS total tab views per user actually grew (+11.5%).

Works only on free users. doesn't affect ex subscribers

on iOS we lose about 30% of received gain in revenue

Overall recommendation: can roll out on both platforms with post roll out control of churn and next year recurrent rate for intro plans

Conclusion
[macro]
Red fail
[/macro]

Do not roll out as-is.

As a result, our trial sign-up rate increased (30-86% in different segments), but our conversion rate to paid subscriptions decreased (36-54%).

The quality of trials is declining significantly.

Free users are in the black by $685. And we are losing another $320 on advertising, so in fact we are in the black by $365 per day, giving everyone free access for two weeks.

The daily/ad-fail interstitial plus banners meaningfully increases access conversion and trial share

but it appears to push lower-intent users into trials: downstream monetization quality drops (trial→charge declines on Android; similar effects on iOS)

and iOS retention (7d/14d) and session-depth rates fall. 

The 5s close delay likely adds friction, reducing perceived UX quality and diluting trial quality.

Notably, Long-trials cohorts show strong ARPU/charge CR lifts on iOS (and better access CR and AOV/ARPPU on Android), suggesting potential when user intent is high, but current broad targeting degrades overall monetization quality.

on iOS most of revenue increase came from ex subscribers, while free users have minimal impact

Next steps

Waiting for the unified_id fix

Re-tune interstitial: shorten/instant close, reduce default emphasis on free trial (promote direct purchase or clearer terms), and/or cap frequency (e.g., ad-fail only or high-intent users).

especially on Android with current ~10% trial → charge conversion  

Re-test with KPIs: ARPU, trial→charge, 7d retention.
