# Pitch

## Context & Idea

Goal: Increase ARPU  by selling Pro+ through both interstitials as an instant discounted purchase (no trial, no intro ramp), and test whether this brings higher-quality subscribers. Both prior tests grew via trials/discounts but hurt quality (early cancels +69–118%, AOV/ARPPU −9–14%).

Foundation : Today free users get an intro plan and ex-subscribers get a fresh trial before the full price. We replace the offer on both screens with a single instant discounted plan. Same triggers and creative — only the offer changes.

Idea: Follow-up to two live tests (UMN-9389 free-paywall, UMN-9259 winback), whose own next steps called for "drop the trial, promote direct purchase." Reuse existing screens, funnel and analytics; only the offer changes. Goal metric — ARPU; watch early cancels/refunds, AOV/ARPPU, recurrence.

### Questions & Answers

## Confidence research

UG App: paywall – offer instead of ad interstitials (UMN-9389). Free-user interstitial. The 14-day-trial version failed on quality (trial→charge −36–54%), so we switched to an intro plan and it worked (ARPU +11.5% iOS / +25% Android). Key learning: the trial pulls in low-intent users; the explicit next step was "reduce emphasis on free trial, promote direct purchase."

UG App: winback – add interstitials for former subscribers (UMN-9259). Ex-subscriber interstitial with a fresh 14-day trial → $39.99. Strong ARPU (+42.8%) but early cancellations spiked +69–118% — the trial attracted re-trialists who churn fast. Same quality problem, same lever to test.

UG App: tour install – discount for young users (UMN-10085). Most relevant precedent: we gave a cheap instant plan to a narrow segment (under-25) with no trial. Conversion in the offer funnel jumped 2.5–3x, revenue from that source +49–105%, ARPU for it +50%, and cancel/refund rates did not rise — often fell (charge→14d refund −41%). This is direct evidence that a cheaper instant price raises conversion without degrading quality. In our new project we go one step further — an even cheaper instant offer on the two high-traffic interstitial surfaces (all free users + ex-subscribers, not just under-25).

## Reach & Impact

#### Reach — restricted to tour-install-during-run (per current Solution scope)

Eligible = users who tour-installed during the experiment and hit the interstitial. ~12,950 new users/day: iOS 14Free ~6,143, iOS Winback ~504, Android 14Free ~5,983, Android Winback ~323. Baselines: net, mature cohort 2026-06-01..06-23; UGT_IOS/UGT_ANDROID only; attributed by funnel_source. ~4–12× smaller than the full interstitial pool — this is what limits power (see design).

#### Reach & Impact — funnel (A / B / Diff, instant $29.99 model)

Baseline (A) recomputed on the correct eligibility: users who tour-installed during the run (default.ug_installs) and then saw the interstitial — not the full interstitial pool. New-install users convert ~2–4× better, so the earlier full-pool baseline understated CR and ARPU. Cohort 2026-06-01..06-23 (mature, pending trials = 0); net revenue. B (test) models the instant $29.99 offer: 14Free keeps conversion flat (price +50% lifts ARPPU); Winback removes the trial (subscribe = charge), modelled as members → buyer +88% at ARPPU $29.99 × store commission.

Winback control funnel decomposition (why buyers are few today): members → trial start iOS 7.2% / Android 9.5%, then trial → charge 19.5% / 17.7%. The instant test removes the leaky trial step.

#### Impact — forecast

Steady-state surface revenue on the restricted audience ≈ $463/day (ARPU × eligible/day); ARPU goal +10% → +$46/day. Small because the eligible audience is tiny — value is validating the offer for later full rollout.

⚠️ Price shift both ways:

Winback DOWN $39.99→$29.99 (−25%)+trial removed → ARPPU falls, CR must rise >33% to break even.

14Free UP $19.99-intro→$29.99-instant (+50% first payment) → ARPPU rises, CR/volume may fall.

## Hypothesis

If we replace the current trial / intro-discount offers with a single instant discounted Pro+ plan for all free users (paywall interstitial) and former subscribers (winback interstitial) in the daily-interstitial and ad-fail scenarios , we expect ARPU to increase by at least XX% without a drop in subscriber quality (charge→14d cancel/refund and 7-day retention) , because an instant paid purchase — with no trial cushion or discount ramp — self-selects higher-intent buyers, as shown by the tour-install discount test where a cheaper instant offer lifted conversion 2.5–3x with cancel/refund rates flat or lower.

#### Recommendation

Powerable in ~3 weeks — the earlier “unpowerable / ~100 days” verdict was an artefact of the mismeasured baseline. On the correct new-install cohort conversion is 2–4× higher, so required samples shrink sharply. Binding duration ≈ 20 days (Winback conversion is the long pole); the Total-ARPU goal and 14Free read in ~1 week.

Primary goal = Total ARPU (whole eligible surface). Instant $29.99 lifts it +61% / +67% (iOS / Android), powering in ~4–7 days , driven by 14Free (ARPPU +62% / +67% at roughly flat conversion). Treat 14Free members → buyer as a guardrail (expected flat; watch it does not drop under the +50% price).

Winback = the long pole. members → buyer CR (+88% expected) powers in ~15–20 days . Winback ARPPU falls by design (price −25% + trial removed), so judge Winback on ARPU/quality, not ARPPU.

Keep 3 arms (A / B / C). Bonferroni α/2 = 0.025 is already inside the estimate; pool iOS + Android only if a Winback cell lags.

The new-install eligibility is fine — it raises intent/quality and does NOT gut power (the opposite of the earlier read). No need to ask the DRI to lift it.

Expected impact: +$747/day surface revenue at 100% rollout on the eligible audience (+63%), driven by 14Free. Goal = Total ARPU (CUPED). Guardrails: charge → 14d cancel/refund, 7-day retention, ARPPU/AOV (down by design on Winback), trial share.

## Decision

Do not roll out this iteration on either platform. Keep the concept alive for iOS. Final call is the DRI's.

iOS — hold and re-run, do not discard. On the 14Free interstitial the instant offer lifted net revenue by roughly +58% at flat subscription volume, and that comparison is trial-free and fully charged on both sides, so it is a final read rather than a preliminary one. It is still not significant (p=0.22 on surface ARPU) because the run delivered 9 of the 15 designed days.

Android — no case for the $29.99 step. The higher effective first payment was almost exactly cancelled by a conversion loss, leaving surface revenue slightly negative. This is price elasticity, not execution: same offer, same creative, same triggers as iOS.

Winback interstitial — undecided, not a negative result. Its control branch is entirely trial-based and most of those trials have not reached their charge date, so control revenue on that surface is still incomplete and the comparison understates it.

The headline Forecast understates the change and should not drive the call. The projected rollout effect is about −$215/day on iOS and −$110/day on Android, but it is built on Total ARPU, which averages the treated interstitials (12% of iOS and 17% of Android cohort revenue) together with the untouched surfaces that supply the rest and drifted independently.

Nothing can be concluded about subscriber quality yet. Pending 14d charges sit far above the 5% gate in every branch, so churn and refund movements — including the large nominal Android churn number — are not interpretable at this snapshot.

### Next steps

Re-run on iOS with the interstitial surface as the primary metric. The design's own baseline was the surface ARPU, but the experiment was judged on all-surface Total ARPU — a base roughly 8× wider than the change, which cannot resolve it at any realistic sample.

Run the re-test to the designed exposure. This iteration stopped at 9 of 15 designed days on iOS and under half the designed Android sample, and shipped 2 of the 3 planned variations.

Revisit the Winback surface after 2026-08-04 , once the control trials' charge window has closed, and re-read charge quality (churn and refund 14d) at the same time.

On Android, test a smaller price step before repeating $29.99. The conversion loss, not the ticket size, is the binding constraint there.

Do not reuse Total ARPU as the goal metric for surface-scoped paywall tests. Size and judge them on the segment that the treatment actually reaches.

## Insights

### Insight summary

The offer worked on the surface it changed, on iOS. On the 14Free interstitial the instant $29.99 plan lifted net revenue 58.14% on iOS ($353.87 → $559.62) at flat subscription volume (28 → 29). This surface carries no trial in either variation and every subscription has already charged, so the number is final, not preliminary.

On Android the same offer is a wash, because conversion is price-elastic. The effective first payment rose 70.89% ($14.57 → $24.90 net per subscription) but subscription volume fell -44.44%, leaving surface revenue -5.06%. On iOS the effective payment rose a comparable 52.69% and volume did not fall - the platforms differ in elasticity, not in the offer.

The goal metric could not have detected this. The two interstitials produce only 12.20% of iOS and 17.40% of Android revenue in the control branch. Total ARPU therefore averages the treated surface with the 87.80% / 82.60% of revenue the experiment never touched, where revenue drifted -6.56% on iOS and -21.51% on Android for reasons unrelated to the treatment.

The Winback interstitial is not yet readable. Its control branch is entirely trial-based: on iOS 32 of 32 control subscriptions are trials and only 4 have charged, with 18 still inside their charge window; on Android 0 of 24 have charged. Control revenue on this surface will keep rising, so today's Winback comparison understates control.

### Supporting questions

### Did the instant discounted offer make more money on the surface it replaced?

Answer. Yes on iOS, no on Android. Confirmatory.

Proof.

iOS 14Free interstitial, net revenue $353.87 → $559.62 (58.14%); surface ARPU $0.050 → $0.080.

Android 14Free interstitial, net revenue $262.28 → $249.00 (-5.06%); surface ARPU $0.040 → $0.038.

Neither variation uses a trial on this surface and every subscription has charged, so no charge-window maturity caveat applies to this comparison.

The package Interstitials segment agrees on direction: ARPU 41.65% on iOS (p=0.22) and 34.17% on Android (p=0.45) - positive but not significant at the delivered sample.

### What is the mechanism - price, or the removal of the trial?

Answer. Price. The trial removal worked as designed but the outcome is driven by how each platform absorbs a higher effective first payment. Confirmatory.

Proof.

Trials share on the interstitial surface collapsed as intended: iOS 54.10% → 6.45% (p=0.000), Android 52.50% → 17.65% (p=0.004).

Effective net revenue per subscription on the 14Free interstitial rose on both platforms - iOS $12.64 → $19.30 (52.69%), Android $14.57 → $24.90 (70.89%) - because the replaced control plans charged a reduced intro amount, not their headline price.

Volume responded in opposite directions: iOS subscriptions 28 → 29 (3.57%), Android 18 → 10 (-44.44%). Same offer, same creative, same triggers, so the difference is platform price elasticity.

Quality proxies on the surface moved the right way where they are readable: iOS cheapskaters 15.15% → 0.00% (p=0.015), and AOV 55.60% on iOS / 83.08% on Android.

### Why does Total ARPU read flat-to-negative when the treated surface improved on iOS?

Answer. Because the treated surface is a small minority of the revenue the goal metric averages over. Confirmatory.

Proof.

In the control branch the two interstitials generate 12.20% of iOS revenue and 17.40% of Android revenue attributed to the cohort.

Revenue on the untouched surfaces - 87.80% of iOS and 82.60% of the Android total - moved -6.56% and -21.51% respectively. The treatment does not act there, so this is drift, but it is large enough to dominate the Total figure.

Result: Total ARPU -5.37% on iOS (p=0.61) and -7.08% on Android (p=0.73).

The design baseline for the goal metric ($0.11 iOS / $0.071 Android) is close to the measured interstitial-surface ARPU, not to the measured Total-segment ARPU ($0.53 iOS / $0.22 Android). The experiment was sized for the surface metric but judged on the all-surface metric.

### Did losing interstitial subscriptions push users to other paywalls?

Answer. No measurable recovery. Exploratory.

Proof.

Subscriptions on untouched surfaces moved from 291 to 266 on iOS and 157 to 154 on Android - flat to slightly down, not up.

Engagement guardrails are flat on both platforms (app retention 7d -0.88% iOS / -0.59% Android; tab-view events per user -0.79% iOS), so the variation did not change who reaches downstream surfaces.

Labelled exploratory: the untouched-surface movement is not a treatment effect and its direction is within the noise this sample supports.

### Is any charge-quality or churn conclusion available yet?

Answer. No. The charge window is still open across every branch. Confirmatory.

Proof.

pending 14d charges share is far above the 5% gate in all branches: iOS 61.26% control / 59.60% test, Android 78.79% / 71.88%.

Churn 14d and refund 14d are therefore not interpretable - the nominal Android churn move 123.44% (p=0.07) sits on a base that is mostly still pending.

The Winback control branch is the clearest case: on iOS 4 of 32 subscriptions have charged with 18 still pending, and on Android 0 of 24.

The 14Free interstitial comparison is exempt: it is trial-free on both sides and fully charged, which is why it is the one surface with a final read.

### Was the experiment delivered as designed?

Answer. No - it ran to roughly half the planned exposure and one variation short. Confirmatory.

Proof.

Duration: 9 days delivered against a binding design of 15 days on iOS and 20 days on Android.

Sample per variation: iOS 7,075 / 7,043 against a design of 8,511; Android 6,586 / 6,622 against a design of 14,644 - Android reached under half its planned sample.

The design specified three variations (A / B / C) with Bonferroni correction; the admin ran two. A/B balance itself is fine (under 1% on both platforms).

This is why directionally large surface effects land at p-values between 0.2 and 0.5 rather than resolving.
