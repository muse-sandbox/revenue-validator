Pitch

Context & Idea

  • Goal: Increase incremental ARPU per former subscriber by monetizing users who exhausted the standard winback interstitial window without converting. We're doing this now because the impression cap project has revealed a clear gap: after 2–5 shows, the standard winback interstitial stops converting — leaving a warm, high-intent segment completely unmonetized.
  • Foundation: Currently, former subscribers who exhaust the effective winback interstitial window (2–5 impressions) without purchasing fall back to regular ad monetization — the same as any free user. There is no dedicated final attempt to re-engage them with a stronger incentive. This is suboptimal because this segment has already demonstrated purchase intent by previously paying for a subscription, making them significantly more likely to convert than standard free users (AD Winback CR is 2–4% vs <0.2% for free users). The fix: introduce a single final interstitial with a deeply discounted offer shown only once, after the standard winback window is exhausted.

Questions & Answers

  • Does the offer effectiveness differ by time since churn?
    • We're targeting all former subscribers who exhausted the standard winback window as a single segment.
    • However, users who churned recently (0–3 months) may have different price elasticity compared to those who churned 12+ months ago.
    • We plan to analyze conversion by time-since-churn cohort post-experiment to validate this assumption and inform whether future iterations should use churn recency as a targeting signal instead of impression count.

Confidence research

1. Internal data: winback interstitials are highly effective for former subscribers

The project [2025-08-01] UG App: winback – add interstitials for former subscribers demonstrated that this format is among the most efficient monetization touchpoints for lapsed users:

  • iOS Variation 2 delivered ARPU +42.8%, access CR +170.9%, charge CR +37.8%

  • Daily revenue grew from $2,365 → $3,379 on iOS

  • Winback access conversion: 3.9%, charge conversion: 0.7%

  • AD Winback CR is 2–4% vs. <0.2% for AD 14Free — 10–20x higher

This confirms former subscribers are the most convertible segment in our funnel.

2. Internal data: the effective impression window is limited to the first 2–5 shows

The project [2026-04-13] UG App: reduction in advertising volume established:

  • 60% of all interstitial conversions happen on the 1st impression

  • On iOS, 90% of conversions occur within the first 2 impressions

  • On Android, 90% of conversions occur within the first 6 impressions

  • Beyond these thresholds, additional impressions deliver no meaningful subscription value

This directly implies that users who did not convert after 2–5 winback interstitials need a fundamentally different offer — not a repeat of the same one.

3. Industry pattern: "last chance" + discount works for lapsed paid users

Hulu uses a final-call strategy with explicit urgency — "$2.99/Month — LAST CHANCE" — specifically targeting former paid subscribers (not trial users), based on the reasoning that this audience has higher purchase intent and is statistically more likely to convert.

RevenueCat highlights in its winback playbook that discounts are most effective when reserved for high-intent users who have already gone through the standard offer cycle — and recommends applying them only after the average non-discounted conversion window has passed.

4. Cannibalization risk is low given the correct offer sequencing

A key principle from both RevenueCat research and our internal data: a discount should never precede the standard offer. In our mechanic, the final interstitial is shown only after the user has already declined the $40/year offer across 2–5 impressions. This significantly reduces the risk of training users to wait for a cheaper deal, since the full-price offer is always presented first and the discounted final call is a one-time, terminal touchpoint.

Description of the Solution & Mockups

Solution

  • The experiment targets former subscribers who previously had a Pro subscription, no longer have an active subscription

  • The new interstitial will be available after the winback interstitial has been viewed, but no sooner than the following day

  • The new interstitial displays a promo code that users can swipe to reveal and get a discount

    • We offer users a $19.99 plan on the paywall

  • The new interstitial can be viewed only once

  • How it works:

    • The user views the winback interstitial

    • A view marker is assigned to them and linked to exp 7487 → we change the iteration -> the old markers are no longer relevant

    • Next, the experiment with the final interstitial launches

    • When the user visits the tab, a check runs: did they view the winback interstitial, and if so, was it yesterday?

      • If not: the user is on the legacy logic

      • If both are yes: do they have variation B?

        • If yes - show the final offer and send App Experiment Start

        • If not - show the winback interstitial and send App Experiment Start

Decision Greensuccess

Can roll out variation 2 (the one-time $19.99 final interstitial) on both UGT_ANDROID and UGT_IOS, with active monitoring on iOS.

  • no significant increase on arpu but only due to small segments
  • Android — clear win, roll out. No cannibalization.
  • iOS — roll out, but watch full-price cannibalization. 
  • Trade-off is by design. The offer converts trials into immediate payment, lowering AOV/ARPPU (iOS AOV −14.4%, p=0.001) — expected, since $19.99 < the ~$40/yr plan. It does not affect the standard winback CR (shown only after the winback window is exhausted).

Next steps

  1. Roll out variation 2 on Android at 100% — clean incremental win.
  2. Roll out variation 2 on iOS, with a post-rollout watch on non-winback / full-price conversion (Total − Winbacks) to confirm the diffuse dilution stays bounded and the net stays positive.
  3. Iterate iOS targeting to cut the dilution — e.g. tighten eligibility so the $19.99 offer reaches only genuinely exhausted users (exclude those who recently viewed a full-price paywall or are mid-trial).
  4. Recheck churn 14d / refund 14d once the control 14d charge windows close (currently preliminary — pending-charge share > 5% on control branches) to confirm the cheaper product carries no late retention/refund penalty.

Insights

Summary

Experiment 7487 — [UG Monetization] UG App: winback - final interstitial offer. Clients: UGT_IOS, UGT_ANDROID (v7.3.9). Period: 2026-05-22 → 2026-06-12 (~21 days). Variation 1 = control (users who exhausted the winback window fall back to regular ad monetization); Variation 2 = test (one-time final interstitial with a promo-code paywall at $19.99). The Total and Winbacks (funnel_source in ('AD Winback Interstitial','AD Final Interstitial')) segments share the same members, so Total − Winbacks is a clean "everything except the winback interstitial" split.

Maturity (updated): all trials have now matured — pending trials = 0 in every branch — so trial → charge is now final. ARPU / Revenue / Buyers / member→subscriber/buyer conversions are mature. churn 14d / refund 14d remain preliminary: 14d pending-charge share is still above 5% on the control branches (iOS Winbacks ctrl 52%, Android Total ctrl 19%, Android Winbacks ctrl 47%, iOS Total ctrl 6.9%; all test branches ≤ 6%).

What changed since the pre-maturity read: as control trials matured into charges, control buyers and revenue rose, so the test's relative lift shrank. The conversion win on the targeted winback segment holds, but the total-account ARPU/Revenue lift is no longer statistically significant on either platform.

  • The $19.99 offer revives a previously dead segment. On the exhausted-winback segment the offer significantly raises member→buyer / member→subscriber conversion on both platforms. iOS Winbacks: buyers 21 → 96, member→buyers +365.6% (p=0.000), ARPU +241% (p=0.000). Android Winbacks: buyers 17 → 35, member→buyers +103.8% (p=0.013); Android Winback ARPU +34% is positive but not significant (p=0.33) after the cheaper price and control maturation.
  • From the first interstitial show, the new surface wins on paid conversion. member→buyer on the entry interstitial — AD Final Interstitial (test) vs AD Winback Interstitial (control): iOS 0.14% → 0.93% (+562%, p<0.001), Android 0.37% → 0.78% (+113%, p=0.02). The win is on payment, not on subscription starts: the winback interstitial mostly opens trials that rarely charge, while the final interstitial is immediate payment.
  • At the total-account level the lift is positive but not significant. iOS Total ARPU +9.3% (p=0.40), Android Total ARPU +26.5% (p=0.22). The earlier-looking significance disappeared once control trials charged.
  • iOS shows dilution; Android is incremental but underpowered. iOS non-winback buyers fell 167 → 140 (−27) and non-winback revenue −$702, largely offsetting the +$998 winback revenue gain (net total → +$296). Android non-winback buyers grew 36 → 44 (+8), non-winback revenue +$221 (net total → +$395), consistent with no cannibalization — but the small sample leaves it short of significance.
  • The offer trades trials for immediate payment (now mature). Trial share among subscriptions collapses: iOS Winbacks 90% → 27%, Android Winbacks 80% → 48%. AOV/ARPPU fall by design ($19.99 < the standard ~$40/year): iOS Total AOV −14.4% (p=0.001), ARPPU −14.5% (p=0.000); iOS Winbacks AOV −26.7% (p=0.013). With trials fully matured, test trial → charge is significantly lower on Android (Total 31.4% → 5.9%, p=0.004; Winbacks 28.1% → 6.7%, p=0.019) — the offer pulls high-intent users into an immediate purchase, leaving fewer, lower-intent trial-takers.
  • Standard winback CR is structurally protected. The final offer is shown only after the standard winback window is exhausted and no sooner than the next day, so it cannot affect the winback interstitial's own conversion.

The $19.99 offer turns the exhausted-winback segment into direct paying buyers: instant (non-trial) purchases surge, trial share collapses. iOS Winbacks: Instant Accesses 8 → 91, Trials 72 → 33, Buyers 21 → 96, Revenue $424 → $1,422; member→buyers 0.21% → 0.98% (+365.6%, p=0.000). Android Winbacks: Instant Accesses 5 → 32, Buyers 17 → 35, Revenue $495 → $669; member→buyers 0.48% → 0.97% (+103.8%, p=0.013).

3. Where did iOS non-winback purchases go? (exploratory)

The iOS non-winback decline is not concentrated on a single surface — it is spread thinly across many organic/feature sources (transpose, Smartscroll, autoscroll, Official Tabs Search, …). This reads as mild diffuse dilution rather than cannibalization of one specific full-price offer. (A direct join of subscriptions by funnel_source over-counts absolutes vs the package totals, so only the relative pattern is used, not absolute numbers.)

4. Trial → immediate payment trade (now mature)

The no-trial offer shifts the subscription mix toward immediate payment: trial share iOS Total 34.1% → 16.7%, Android Total 45.5% → 30.6%; on the winback segment iOS 90% → 27%, Android 80% → 48%. Now that pending trials = 0, test trial → charge is significantly lower on Android (Total 31.4% → 5.9%, p=0.004; Winbacks 28.1% → 6.7%, p=0.019) and directionally lower on iOS (Total 19.1% → 15.2%, p=0.56) — the offer captures high-intent users up front, so the residual trial-takers convert worse. On this high-intent segment the immediate-$19.99 trade is still EV-positive; lower AOV/ARPPU is a direct consequence of the cheaper product, by design.
