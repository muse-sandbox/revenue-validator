I run a research based on First session 3rd iteration to check what happened with users retention and does current idea could fix that. 

1. Does the decline persist after data validation?

I checked data maturity, randomization, cohort definitions, and user composition. After switching to the full bucketed cohort, the difference on retention persisted: −1.60 pp on iOS and −2.26 pp on Android. The earlier conclusion that "there is no effect on Android" turned out to be a consequence of a biased cohort. The change in user composition does not explain the observed difference and, per the decomposition, partially masks it.

2. Do the data support the awareness-gap hypothesis?

Variation C gave the user more information and choice but did not improve retention relative to B. Other available signals also do not show that additional demonstration of capabilities increases conversion. Therefore the original hypothesis has not been confirmed so far.

3. In which segment is the difference concentrated?

The ARPU growth is driven primarily by higher conversion, not by the average check. Within the first three days, about 7% of B users subscribed — they retain better than average. Among the 81% who reached the paywall but did not purchase, Retention 14d is 1.79 pp lower than the equivalent control segment. Among the remaining 12% who did not reach the paywall, no statistically distinguishable difference was found.

The segments are defined by behavior after entering the experiment: the result shows where the difference is concentrated, but does not prove that the paywall caused it.

4. Is the retention harm uniform across user levels? (exp 7622, tour-level split)

User level is a pre-treatment covariate, so between-arm comparisons within a level are clean.

  • On iOS the entire retention damage is concentrated in Intermediate: Retention 14d −4.9 pp (p < 0.001) with zero purchase gain. This is the only result that survives correction for multiplicity (24 comparisons); the other segment-level signals are directional.

  • The purchase lift is concentrated in Beginner: day-0 purchases +1.62 pp (5.43% → 7.05%, p = 0.001). The chooser (variation C) returns Beginner retention to the control level (+2.08 pp, p = 0.034).

  • On Android both Beginner (−3.15 pp, p = 0.001) and Intermediate (−3.49 pp, p = 0.016) lose retention and the chooser helps neither — an additional argument for an iOS-only launch.

  • Consequence: we keep a single flow and use level only as an analytical slice in the readout; the primary decision is made on overall iOS ARPU and Retention 14d.

5. Where does the day-0 funnel lose users?

  • The format-choice step costs about 1% of users at every level. One more screen shown as a continuation of the setup is cheap, which supports collapsing the feature demonstration into the setup path instead of adding standalone demo screens.

  • The largest drop is Paywall 2 → 60 seconds with the tab: 34–40% of users never get there. The post-dismiss chooser significantly improves this transition (Beginner 61.5% → 65.8%, Intermediate 65.7% → 72.8%), so after a paywall dismiss we keep the chooser and add no repeated pressure.

6. Which locked features do users actually touch, by chosen format?

Day-0 taps on locked Pro features (a tap fires a Paywall View with that feature’s value), split by the answer to “How do you want to play?”. About 78% of users choose Chords and 22% choose Tabs; the ranges cover variations B and C.

  • For Tabs users demand sits in locked playback: Smartscroll + Autoscroll account for ≈40% of touches, and Autoscroll is the only feature where Tabs users lead Chords users (+6 pp). Simplify and Transpose serve Chords users.

  • Consequence for the flow: the setup branches by format. Chords users get one compact Simplify / Transpose setup screen; for Tabs users no paid-catalog setting has meaningful demand, so their branch goes straight to a paywall that leads with playback value.

  • Secondary slice by level: Simplify is a beginner feature (14.5% Beginner → 10.9% Intermediate → 5.2% Advanced); Transpose peaks at Intermediate (16.8%) and, per the format split, belongs to Chords users adjusting the key for singing. Smartscroll demand is highest among Beginner (33.3%).

  • Caveat: the touch proxy sees only locked Pro features; demand for free tools (tempo, loop) is invisible to it.
