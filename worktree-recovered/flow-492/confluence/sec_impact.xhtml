Room for change. The B lift is volume-driven (conversion, not the average check): members → subscribers is ≈ 7%, nowhere near a ceiling, so holding the conversion gain is realistic; the metric with the real risk is the retention guardrail.

A/B model = forecast (iOS, per day at 100% rollout, 7,699 exposed users / day, from the iteration-3 Forecast):

  • control flow: ≈ $5,080 / day (7,699 × $0.66);
  • new variation at B-level monetization: ≈ $5,960 / day (+17.25%, preliminary snapshot) up to ≈ $6,240 / day (+22.8%, mature reading);
  • incremental: ≈ +$880…+$1,160 / day (≈ +$0.32–0.42M / year) — conditional on ARPU holding at the B level while the setup path restores retention.

Rationale. The test reuses the variation-B funnel (official tab → Paywall 2) and only replaces the passive demonstration with a setup path, so the conversion mechanism is preserved; the day-0 funnel research shows one extra setup-style screen costs ≈ 1% of users per step (What I checked, item 5), which the personalized paywall is expected to offset. The demo screens target the retention harm concentrated in paywall non-purchasers (−1.79 pp, 81% of the cohort) and Intermediate users (What I checked, items 3–4). Main risk: the setup step dampens paywall pressure and ARPU regresses toward control — hence the goal is holding the B level, not a further lift.

No additional traffic is expected from the change (first-session flow only, no acquisition effect).
