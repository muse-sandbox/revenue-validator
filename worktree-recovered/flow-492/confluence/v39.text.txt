Pitch
Context & Idea
Goal.
Удержать рост конверсии предыдущего экспериимента по первой сессии, не уронив ретеншн.
Foundation.
The relaunched guided first-song flow increased total ARPU by sending more users into the Official Tabs purchase funnel. The gain was volume-driven: trial-to-charge and AOV did not improve, while D1 retention and tab-view engagement declined in Variation 2.
Idea.
Reuse iteration 3 variation B, after showing an official tab add the feature demonstration screens with a setup path and then show Paywall 2.
Questions & Answers
How many capabilities we should show ?
One compact setup surface shown as a continuation of the setup: Simplify + Transpose for Chords users, none for Tabs users (their demand sits in locked playback and is addressed on the paywall). See What I checked, items 4–6.
Which capabilities enter the setup path?
Answered by the feature-demand research (What I checked, items 4–6): the setup branches by chosen format — Chords users get one compact Simplify + Transpose screen; Tabs users get no paid-catalog setup step and go to a paywall that leads with playback value (Smartscroll / Autoscroll). Selection criterion stays: the setting must produce value in the free session, not advertise a paid feature.
Do we need a third arm (variation B as is) for a direct mechanism comparison?
Open design question.
Retention guardrail threshold.
Blocking; requires the acceptable Retention 14d decrease to be fixed (Decision needed, item 2).
Confidence research
Prior evidence: the #7622 first-session research below. Statistical robustness of the prior evidence:
The Retention 14d decline is significant on the full bucketed cohort: iOS B −1.60 pp (p = 0.004), Android B −2.26 pp (p = 7·10⁻⁵), Android C −2.39 pp (p = 2.4·10⁻⁵). In the dominant segment — users who reached the paywall without purchasing (81% of the cohort) — the decline is −1.79 pp (p = 0.0037).
The ARPU lift is reproduced in two independent mature readings: +22.8% (p = 0.011) and +24.2% (p = 0.001). It comes from conversion (members → subscribers +13.1%), not from the average check (AOV +3.1%, not significant).
Randomization at the exposure boundary is clean: no sample ratio mismatch was detected.
Composition decomposition: variation B brings +0.75 pp more early subscribers, and they retain better than average, so the composition shift masks the harm (its contribution is −0.23 pp); the mix-adjusted decline is −1.82 pp, larger than the raw one.
What I checked
I run a research based on
First session 3rd iteration
to check what happened with users retention and does current idea could fix that.
1. Does the decline persist after data validation?
I checked data maturity, randomization, cohort definitions, and user composition. After switching to the full bucketed cohort, the difference on retention persisted: −1.60 pp on iOS and −2.26 pp on Android. The earlier conclusion that "there is no effect on Android" turned out to be a consequence of a biased cohort. The change in user composition does not explain the observed difference and, per the decomposition, partially masks it.
2. Do the data support the awareness-gap hypothesis?
Variation C gave the user more information and choice but did not improve retention relative to B. Other available signals also do not show that additional demonstration of capabilities increases conversion. Therefore the original hypothesis has not been confirmed so far.
3. In which segment is the difference concentrated?
The ARPU growth is driven primarily by higher conversion, not by the average check. Within the first three days, about 7% of B users subscribed — they retain better than average. Among the 81% who reached the paywall but did not purchase, Retention 14d is 1.79 pp lower than the equivalent control segment. Among the remaining 12% who did not reach the paywall, no statistically distinguishable difference was found.
The segments are defined by behavior after entering the experiment: the result shows where the difference is concentrated, but does not prove that the paywall caused it.
4. Is the retention harm uniform across user levels? (exp 7622, tour-level split)
User level is a pre-treatment covariate, so between-arm comparisons within a level are clean.
On iOS the entire retention damage is concentrated in Intermediate: Retention 14d −4.9 pp (p < 0.001) with zero purchase gain. This is the only result that survives correction for multiplicity (24 comparisons); the other segment-level signals are directional.
The purchase lift is concentrated in Beginner: day-0 purchases +1.62 pp (5.43% → 7.05%, p = 0.001). The chooser (variation C) returns Beginner retention to the control level (+2.08 pp, p = 0.034).
On Android both Beginner (−3.15 pp, p = 0.001) and Intermediate (−3.49 pp, p = 0.016) lose retention and the chooser helps neither — an additional argument for an iOS-only launch.
Consequence: we keep a single flow and use level only as an analytical slice in the readout; the primary decision is made on overall iOS ARPU and Retention 14d.
5. Where does the day-0 funnel lose users?
The format-choice step costs about 1% of users at every level. One more screen shown as a continuation of the setup is cheap, which supports collapsing the feature demonstration into the setup path instead of adding standalone demo screens.
The largest drop is Paywall 2 → 60 seconds with the tab: 34–40% of users never get there. The post-dismiss chooser significantly improves this transition (Beginner 61.5% → 65.8%, Intermediate 65.7% → 72.8%), so after a paywall dismiss we keep the chooser and add no repeated pressure.
6. Which locked features do users actually touch, by chosen format?
Day-0 taps on locked Pro features (a tap fires a Paywall View with that feature’s value), split by the answer to “How do you want to play?”. About 78% of users choose Chords and 22% choose Tabs; the ranges cover variations B and C.

Room for change.
The B lift is volume-driven (conversion, not the average check): members → subscribers is ≈ 7%, nowhere near a ceiling, so holding the conversion gain is realistic; the metric with the real risk is the retention guardrail.

Rationale.
The test reuses the variation-B funnel (official tab → Paywall 2) and only replaces the passive demonstration with a setup path, so the conversion mechanism is preserved; the day-0 funnel research shows one extra setup-style screen costs ≈ 1% of users per step (What I checked, item 5), which the personalized paywall is expected to offset. The demo screens target the retention harm concentrated in paywall non-purchasers (−1.79 pp, 81% of the cohort) and Intermediate users (What I checked, items 3–4). Main risk: the setup step dampens paywall pressure and ARPU regresses toward control — hence the goal is holding the B level, not a further lift.
No additional traffic is expected from the change (first-session flow only, no acquisition effect).

The flow exists only for users who choose Chords. Users who choose Tabs get the tab, then the paywall — nothing else. Users who choose Chords get the tab, the demo, and only then the paywall.
The path is linear; there is no way back. Every screen has two exits: tap the feature button or tap Continue (the ✕ on the tooltip switches the screen to the Continue state). Details below.
There is no exit from the flow that bypasses the paywall: a full skip reproduces the variation-B experience (tab → Paywall 2); the exit to Explore is the paywall dismiss.
How the screens work:
- Tab screen. While a step overlay is active, the entire tab UI is disabled except the controls in focus. Taps on disabled elements do nothing.
- Close ✕ on the tooltip: switches the screen to the state with the Continue button, without any action on the page
- Backing track: after the download finishes, the button state and the tooltip are updated and the Continue button is shown.
- Simplify: the button and the block of chords that change are highlighted simultaneously. The user can toggle Simplify on/off multiple times before tapping Continue; everything else stays disabled.
- Play (final demo step): the tab plays for 8 seconds; Pause or any other tap ends the demonstration early and opens the paywall. Tap attempts on disabled elements during Play are collected in analytics.
- Fail-forward. Any component failure auto-completes or skips its screen.
- If a feature is unavailable for the song, the step is skipped. For example, Simplify is unavailable for ~27% of songs.
- Paid features are unlocked for the demo session. All other features remain unavailable.
