# Research and Context
Goal: create mechanisms for returning users to resubscribe immediately after they cancel their auto-renewing subscription.
Foundation: currently, we attempt to resubscribe users only within two weeks before their subscription expires. However, most cancellations of auto-renewals occur within the first week after subscribing. As a result, we miss the opportunity to retain a significant portion of our subscribers. In this project we try to create new scenario for returning users and reduce churn.

### Research results
We've launched several experiments with winback offers, based on the main assumption that the sooner a user sees the winback offer after canceling, the more likely they are to accept it. This way, we hope to bring more users back to the subscription.

Results:
Option B (“Extra trial”) showed the best metrics on conversions and revenue: the additional 30-day trial gave a noticeable uplift at all points in the funnel.
However, in fact, many push notifications did not reach users: on average, only ~50% of sent push notifications were delivered, and the proportion of those who opened the app after a push was low.
The final reach of the splash (first screen) was less than 80% of the target group, and the non-splash share was up to 20% of users.
Conclusion:
Server “send” events were recorded correctly, but actual pushes delivered were much lower than expected. The goal was not met due to poor delivery of push notifications and incorrect accounting for the display of the splash, which caused a significant portion of users to not see the offer and not be refunded.

Goals:
A repeat attempt to adjust the previous similar “win-back” experiment: create a mechanism to bring back iOS and Android users who canceled their Pro+, Pro+Courses+Sing auto-renewal subscriptions - immediately after canceling, send them a push notification and show a splash screen with a time-limited discount offer to bring back at least 5% of those who canceled and increase ARPU.

In a past experiment, we offered users a 6 month trial when they canceled their subscription and the resulting covariance in return to subscription increased from 1.21% to 1.38%. In this experiment, we will try to increase this conversion rate and by expanding the user base (include not only trial but also instant) increase the total revenue

# Hypothesis
If we show the user a splash screen with an offer immediately after they cancel the auto-renewal during a trial or subscription, we will retain ~9% (ios, or ~20% for android) more users (who canceled) in the subscription because the splash screen will feature an offer that is advantageous for the user. By accepting it, the user will either re-enable auto-renewal or switch to a different type of subscription

### Solution
#6863
The experiment will work only for ios
The basic mechanics remain the same as in iteration 2.
In this variation, we want to change the write-off mechanism.
Currently, the upgrade rules are in effect - money is debited immediately, and the user receives a refund for unused days of the previous subscription.
Instead of a targeted discount subscription, we will use a promotion offer for the current subscription, which gives exactly the same discount.
Thus, the app store does not debit the money immediately, and the user does not receive a refund. The debit will occur after the current subscription ends and a new billing cycle begins.
The promotion offer gives a discount for 1 year, we will show this in the copyrights on the paywall.
The Promotion Offer can only be used once.

Conclusion
Strong, targeted winback impact on iOS canceled subscribers: very large lifts in ARPU/LTV are driven by substantially higher access and charge conversion from the winback splash/banner, with small but significant gains in engagement and R7–R14 retention. The trade‑off is expected: discounts reduce AOV/ARPPU and shift users from trials to immediate repurchase (trial share down). The “Without winback accesses” segment shows no significant changes, supporting causality from the treatment. Recommendation: roll out the iOS winback to the eligible canceled-subscriber cohort, while monitoring cancellations/refunds and optimizing discount/copy to recover some per‑payer value without losing the conversion volume.

Summary
Retention “as a return on payment” has been confirmed: access and conversion to payment have grown significantly, ARPU/Revenue has grown, R7–R14 — a small but significant plus.
The data shows an increase in instant purchases and a decrease in the share of trials, i.e., the switch to direct purchases has occurred, but with a lower average check.
The value of the mechanism lies in the volume of returns through discounted repurchases.
Insights
The strength of the offer is decisive: a large discount and an exact SKU match result in conversion; a “6-month trial” (previously) was not motivating, but a direct discount is.
The “immediately after cancellation” timing works: increased access to the offer and the checkout chain confirms the hypothesis of instant contact.
Winback increases ARPU and total revenue due to the volume of conversions, but reduces AOV/ARPPU — here you need to play with the size of the discount.
Content retention is growing slightly: small but statistically significant increases in R7/R14 and tab view metrics.
